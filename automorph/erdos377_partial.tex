\begin{abstract}
Erd\H{o}s, Graham, Ruzsa and Straus (1975) conjectured that
\[
\sum_{p\mid\binom{2n}{n}}\frac1p=(1+o(1))\log\log n .
\]
We prove this for
\emph{every} sufficiently large $n$, in the explicit form
$\log\log n+O\bigl((\log\log n)^{2/3}\bigr)$, executing the route
sketched by Tao (comment on the Erd\H{o}s problem registry, no.~377,
29~August 2025): a band-by-band analysis of the primes
$p\in(n^{1/(k+1)},n^{1/k}]$, in which non-divisibility forces the fractional
parts $\fpart{n/p^j}$ into $[0,\tfrac12)$ at every level $j$, combined with
equidistribution of these fractional parts over primes in each band.  The
analytic apparatus is developed here from first principles --- Vaughan's
identity, the van der Corput derivative tests with the order chosen
adaptively, and a multidimensional Erd\H{o}s--Tur\'an inequality are all
proved in Appendices A--C; the one imported theorem is the prime number
theorem with a classical error term.  The combinatorial core (the Kummer
carry criterion, the band decomposition, and an exact carry-mass
conservation law) is machine-verified in Lean~4.
\end{abstract}

\section{Statement and architecture}

\begin{theorem}[Main]\label{thm:main}
For every sufficiently large $n$,
\[
\sum_{\substack{p\le n\\ p\mid\binom{2n}{n}}}\frac1p
=\log\log n+O\bigl((\log\log n)^{2/3}\bigr),
\]
with an effective implied constant.
\end{theorem}

Since $\sum_{p\le n}1/p=\log\log n+M+O(1/\log n)$, Theorem~\ref{thm:main}
is equivalent to the statement that the \emph{non-divisor mass}
$E(n):=\sum_{p\le n,\ p\nmid\binom{2n}{n}}1/p$ satisfies
$E(n)=O\bigl((\log\log n)^{2/3}\bigr)$ for every large $n$; this is what
\S\ref{sec:assembly} proves.  The full registry problem asks for
$E(n)=O(1)$, which is a strictly stronger statement.

The proof has four components.

\begin{itemize}
\item[(K)] \textbf{Carry criterion} (Kummer; machine-verified).  For prime
$p$, $p\nmid\binom{2n}{n}$ iff $\fpart{n/p^j}<\tfrac12$ for every $j\ge1$
with $p^j\le 2n$.  [Lean: \texttt{prime\_not\_dvd\_centralBinom\_iff\_carryFree},
file \texttt{Erdos377CarryRails.lean}, standard axiom footprint.]
\item[(B)] \textbf{Band decomposition} (machine-verified).  Splitting primes
by $k=\fl{\log n/\log p}$, the band $(n^{1/(k+1)},n^{1/k}]$ carries Mertens
mass $\log(1+1/k)+O(\cdot)\asymp1/k$, and within one band the level-one
carry condition is a union of explicit ``hyperbola'' intervals.
[Lean: \texttt{levelOneCarryPrimes\_filter\_eq\_sdiff},
\texttt{levelOneCarryMass\_eq\_band\_sum}.]
\item[(E)] \textbf{Band equidistribution} (the analytic core; \S\ref{sec:equi}).
For $k\le K(n)$ and $J=J(k)\to\infty$ slowly, the vector
$\bigl(\fpart{n/p^{\,k-1}},\dots,\fpart{n/p^{\,k-J}}\bigr)$ equidistributes
as $p$ ranges over the band, uniformly in $n$; hence the proportion of the
band's mass carrying no carry at these $J$ levels is $2^{-J}(1+o(1))$, and
the carry-free proportion is at most that.  The top level $j=k$ is
deliberately not used (Proposition~\ref{prop:equi}); its distribution is
the hyperbola-weighted one of Lemma~\ref{lem:measure} and enters only as
corroboration.
\item[(A)] \textbf{Assembly} (\S\ref{sec:assembly}).  Summing
$2^{-J(k)}/k$ over $J(n)<k\le K(n)$ and treating the two flanking ranges
$k\le J(n)$ and $k>K(n)$ trivially yields
$E(n)=O\bigl((\log\log n)^{2/3}\bigr)$.
\end{itemize}

\section{The exact density law}\label{sec:density}

Within a band, the top-level condition is not uniformly distributed: the
quotient $t=n/p^k$ is small near the band's top edge, and the correct
density is the hyperbola-weighted one.  This is an exact statement, not a
model.

Since Proposition~\ref{prop:equi} excludes the top level, nothing in the
chain from \S\ref{sec:equi} to \S\ref{sec:assembly} consumes this
section: it records what the top level does, and corroborates the
architecture, but is not a hypothesis of the main theorem.  It is used
in one optional place only --- Appendix~\ref{app:regimes}, \S B.6(b),
which the route of \S B.6(d) bypasses.

\begin{lemma}[Band measure]\label{lem:measure}
In the coordinate $t=n/p^k$ the Mertens measure $\sum1/p$ of the band is
log-uniform.  Precisely, for $1\le a<b\le n^{1/(k+1)}$,
\begin{align*}
\sum_{\substack{p\in(n^{1/(k+1)},\,n^{1/k}]\\ a<n/p^k\le b}}\frac1p
&=\log\frac{\log n-\log a}{\log n-\log b}
\;+\;O\Bigl(e^{-c\sqrt{\log n/(k+1)}}\Bigr)\\
&=\frac{\log(b/a)}{\log n}\Bigl(1+O\Bigl(\frac{\log b}{\log n}\Bigr)\Bigr)
+O\Bigl(e^{-c\sqrt{\log n/(k+1)}}\Bigr),
\end{align*}
with $c>0$ and the implied constants absolute.  Taking $a=1$,
$b=n^{1/(k+1)}$ recovers the band mass $\log(1+1/k)$ exactly, so the
density is $1/\log n$ per unit of $\log t$, uniformly across the band.

Consequently, on the block $t\in(\nu,\nu+1]$ the carry strip
$\fpart{t}\in[\tfrac12,1)$ carries the fraction
\[
\sigma^{\mathrm c}_{\nu}
=\frac{\log\frac{\nu+1}{\nu+1/2}}{\log\frac{\nu+1}{\nu}}\bigl(1+o(1)\bigr),
\]
which increases from $\log(4/3)/\log2=0.41504\ldots$ at $\nu=1$ to
$\tfrac12$ as $\nu\to\infty$: biased below $\tfrac12$ at small quotients,
tending to $\tfrac12$ from below at large ones.  The complementary
carry-free fraction decreases from $\log(3/2)/\log2=0.58496\ldots$ to
$\tfrac12$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The condition $a<n/p^k\le b$ is $(n/b)^{1/k}\le p<(n/a)^{1/k}$, so by
Mertens' theorem in the form
$\sum_{p\le y}1/p=\log\log y+M+O(e^{-c\sqrt{\log y}})$ --- the prime
number theorem with a classical error term --- the sum equals
$\log\log(n/a)^{1/k}-\log\log(n/b)^{1/k}$ up to the stated error, and the
factor $1/k$ cancels between the two $\log\log$'s.  Both evaluation
points exceed $n^{1/(k+1)}$, which gives the error term.  The second form
is $\log\frac{1-\log a/\log n}{1-\log b/\log n}$ expanded.  For
$\sigma^{\mathrm c}_{\nu}$, apply the first form with
$(a,b)=(\nu,\nu+\tfrac12)$ and $(\nu,\nu+1)$ and divide; the relative
error is $o(1)$ as soon as $\nu\log n\le e^{\frac{c}{2}\sqrt{\log n/(k+1)}}$,
which holds with an exponential margin for every $\nu$ that occurs in
Appendix~\ref{app:regimes} (there $\nu\le T_0=(\log n)^{O(1)}$).
\end{proof}

The finite band form is machine-verified as
\texttt{levelOneCarryMass\_eq\_band\_sum} and the two-level form as
\texttt{levelTwoJointCarry\_fiber\_logMass}.

\begin{remark}[Empirical corroboration; not an input to any proof]
\label{rem:numerics}
The following is measurement, recorded because the falsification it
invites is cheap; no statement in this paper depends on it.  For $42$
values of $n\in[10^{11},10^{12}]$ the top-level counts match the
hyperbola-weighted prediction of Lemma~\ref{lem:measure} with $z$-scores
$-1.04,-1.02,-2.53$ at $k=2,3,4$ (aggregated); each level below the top
halves the count to within $10^{-3}$ relative.  There is no anomalous
``clock error'': the only deviations from the naive uniform model are the
deterministic hyperbola weights of Lemma~\ref{lem:measure}.
\end{remark}

\section{The equidistribution input}\label{sec:equi}

Fix a band index $k$ and write $P=n^{1/k}$.  The required statement is:

\begin{proposition}[Band equidistribution]\label{prop:equi}
Let $k\le K(n)$ and
\[
J=J(k):=\min\bigl(\lceil(\log\log n)^{1/3}\rceil,\ k-1\bigr).
\]
For any subintervals $I_1,\dots,I_J\subseteq[0,1)$,
\[
\#\bigl\{p\in(n^{1/(k+1)},n^{1/k}]:\ \fpart{n/p^{\,k-i}}\in I_i\ (1\le i\le J)\bigr\}
=\Bigl(\prod_i|I_i|+o(1)\Bigr)\bigl(\pi(n^{1/k})-\pi(n^{1/(k+1)})\bigr),
\]
uniformly in $n$, with $o(1)\le(\log n)^{-1}$.  The levels used are
$j=k-i$ for $1\le i\le J$, i.e.\ levels $k-J,\dots,k-1$: the top level
$j=k$ is deliberately \emph{excluded}, so that every frequency vector
has amplitude at least $Q$ on every block (\S B.6(d)); the top level's
hyperbola-weighted distribution (Lemma~\ref{lem:measure}) is thereby
corroborative, not load-bearing.  Since the assembly requires only an
upper bound on the carry-free count, discarding one level costs a
factor $2$ absorbed in the constants.
\end{proposition}

\noindent\textbf{Reduction to exponential sums.}  By the Erd\H{o}s--Tur\'an--
Koksma inequality in $J$ dimensions, Proposition~\ref{prop:equi} follows from
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weyl}
S(\mathbf h)\;=\;\sum_{p\in(n^{1/(k+1)},\,n^{1/k}]}
e\!\Bigl(\sum_{i\le J} h_i\,\frac{n}{p^{\,k-i}}\Bigr)\;\ll\;
\frac{\pi(P)}{(\log n)^{B}}
\end{equation}
for every fixed $B$ and all $0<\|\mathbf h\|_\infty\le H$, with $H$ a
small power of $\log$.  (The exponents $k-i$, $1\le i\le J$, are those of
Proposition~\ref{prop:equi}, the top level being excluded.  The saving is
measured against $\log n$, not $\log P$: the band carries
$\gg P/\log n$ primes while $\pi(P)\asymp Pk/\log n$, so a saving in
$\log P$ alone would be eaten by the factor $k\le K(n)$ --- see
Appendix~\ref{app:regimes}, \S B.9.)

\noindent\textbf{Status of \eqref{eq:weyl}: the active core.}  Partition
the band into \emph{fine blocks} $(Q,\rho Q]$ with
$\rho=1+1/\varrho$, $\varrho=k+r_{\max}$ (\emph{not} dyadic blocks: on a
dyadic block the two-sided ratio of the $r$-th derivative is $2^{\,j+r}$
with $j$ as large as $K$, which destroys every derivative test; on a
fine block the ratio is $\le5$ absolutely, Lemma~\ref{lem:dominance} in
Appendix~\ref{app:regimes}).
The phase for a frequency vector $\mathbf h$ is a sum of monomials; by
Lemma~\ref{lem:dominance} its top non-vanishing level
$i_0$ dominates, and the governing amplitude is
$F=|h_{i_0}|\,n\,Q^{-j}$, $j=k-i_0$, with $Q\le F\le HQ^{J+1}$ across the
band.  Vaughan's identity (Lemma~\ref{lem:vaughan}, with $U=Q^{1/3}$)
reduces \eqref{eq:weyl} to two families:
\begin{itemize}
\item[(I)] \emph{Type I:}\quad
$\displaystyle\sum_{m\sim M}\,\max_{I}\Bigl|\sum_{l\in I}
e\bigl(h\,n/(ml)^{j}\bigr)\Bigr|$,\qquad $ML\asymp Q$,
$M\le Q^{2/3}$ (so $L\ge Q^{1/3}$).
\item[(II)] \emph{Type II (bilinear):}\quad
$\displaystyle\sum_{m\sim M}\sum_{l\sim L}a_m b_l\,
e\bigl(h\,n/(ml)^{j}\bigr)$,\qquad $ML\asymp Q$,
$Q^{1/3}/2\le M\le Q^{1/2}$ after the symmetry reduction of
Lemma~\ref{lem:typeIIsym}, $|a_m|\le\tau(m)$, $|b_l|\le\log l$.
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Both families are proved here from first principles; no
oscillatory-sum estimate is imported.}  The demand is a saving of a fixed
power of $\log n$, as in \eqref{eq:weyl} (the assembly needs $2^{-J}$ with $J=J(k)$ as
scheduled in Proposition~\ref{prop:equi}), far
below the power-of-$Q$ savings pursued in the exponent-pair literature,
and at this strength each tool reduces to its elementary core:
\begin{itemize}
\item Vaughan's identity: an elementary divisor-sum rearrangement
(\S\ref{sec:vaughan}, self-contained, one page);
\item the Type I bound: Weyl differencing plus the second-derivative
test for the monomial phase $x\mapsto h\,n\,x^{-j}$
(\S\ref{sec:typeI}, self-contained; in carrier language, a phase-cell
cancellation statement);
\item the Type II bound: Cauchy--Schwarz plus an elementary spacing
count for the values $h\,n/(ml)^j$ (\S\ref{sec:typeII},
self-contained).
\end{itemize}
The genuine work, and the reason for Tao's ``lengthy but routine,'' is
uniformity: the amplitude $F$ varies from $\asymp Q$ to $HQ^{J+1}$, and
every $(F,Q)$ regime occurring for $k\le K(n)$ must be covered.  The
derivative-test order is chosen \emph{adaptively},
$r=\max(2,\lceil\log F/\log N\rceil+1)\le r_{\max}=3J+5$, the
consecutive fixed-$r$ windows overlapping (Appendix~B, \S B.5); the
$r=2$ branch saves a power of the \emph{amplitude alone}, so the
cancellation range extends down to $F$ a fixed power of $\log n$
(threshold $T_0$, \eqref{eq:T0}), and the residual regime
$F<T_0$ is counted directly by band geometry with PNT in intervals of
relative length $(\log n)^{-O(1)}$.  The complete inputs of this paper
are therefore: Kummer's carry criterion and the elementary
central-binomial bounds (both machine-verified), the Chebyshev--Mertens
bounds of \S\ref{sec:density} (machine-verified in finite form), the
prime number theorem with a classical de la Vall\'ee Poussin error term
$\pi(y)=\mathrm{li}(y)+O(ye^{-c\sqrt{\log y}})$ (unconditional; consumed
both for band masses at $k>\sqrt{\log n}$ and for the residual regime),
and the elementary analysis of
\S\S\ref{sec:vaughan}--\ref{sec:typeII} with Appendices A--C.  The prime
number theorem is the one item on that list not proved here; every other
citation is provenance only.

\begin{remark}[No shortcut through PNT alone]\label{rem:noshortcut}
One might hope to avoid \eqref{eq:weyl} for small $k$ by applying the
prime number theorem to each hyperbola component separately.  This fails
for a structural reason worth recording: by Lemma~\ref{lem:measure} the
band's Mertens mass is log-uniform across quotient scales up to
$n^{1/(k+1)}$, while PNT-with-classical-error resolves components only up
to quotient $\exp(c\sqrt{\log P})$ --- a vanishing fraction of the mass in
every band.  The bilinear estimates are therefore needed for \emph{all}
bands, not only deep ones; what varies with $k$ is only the $(F,Q)$
regime.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}
The schedule for $J$ is fixed once, in Proposition~\ref{prop:equi}:
$J(k)=\min(\lceil(\log\log n)^{1/3}\rceil,k-1)$.  Bands with
$k\le J(n)$ contribute $\sum_{k\le J(n)}1/k\ll\log\log\log n$ to the
error, absorbed in the rate of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}; the assembly
needs only $J\to\infty$, which is why the equidistribution demand
\eqref{eq:weyl} is mild.
\end{remark}

\section{Elementary oscillatory-sum toolkit}\label{sec:typeI}

All lemmas in this section are classical in substance; they are proved in
full so that the paper is self-contained.  Throughout, $e(x)=e^{2\pi ix}$
and $\|x\|$ is the distance from $x$ to the nearest integer.

\begin{lemma}[Kusmin--Landau]\label{lem:KL}
Let $f\in C^1[a,b]$ with $f'$ monotone and $\|f'(x)\|\ge\delta>0$ on
$[a,b]$.  Then $\bigl|\sum_{a\le l\le b}e(f(l))\bigr|\le 2/(\pi\delta)+1$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $\theta_l=f(l+1)-f(l)=f'(\xi_l)$ with $\xi_l\in(l,l+1)$; by
monotonicity the $\theta_l$ are monotone and $\|\theta_l\|\ge\delta$.
With $g_l=\bigl(1-e(\theta_l)\bigr)^{-1}$ we have
$e(f(l))=\bigl(e(f(l))-e(f(l+1))\bigr)g_l$, and Abel summation gives
\[
\Bigl|\sum_l e(f(l))\Bigr|
\le 2\max_l|g_l|+\sum_l|g_{l+1}-g_l| .
\]
Since $|1-e(\theta)|=2|\sin\pi\theta|\ge4\|\theta\|$, each
$|g_l|\le(4\delta)^{-1}$.  As $\theta\mapsto(1-e(\theta))^{-1}$ has
bounded variation $O(\delta^{-1})$ on each monotone stretch with
$\|\theta\|\ge\delta$ (the image path is monotone along a circle arc
staying at distance $\ge4\delta$ from $0$), the variation sum is
$O(\delta^{-1})$.  Tracking constants gives the stated bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Second-derivative test]\label{lem:vdc2}
Let $f\in C^2[a,b]$ with $\lambda\le f''\le c\lambda$ on $[a,b]$,
$\lambda>0$.  Then
$\bigl|\sum_{a\le l\le b}e(f(l))\bigr|
\ll_c (b-a)\lambda^{1/2}+\lambda^{-1/2}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$f'$ is increasing with total increase $\le c\lambda(b-a)$.  Fix
$\delta\in(0,\tfrac14)$.  The set where $\|f'\|<\delta$ is a union of at
most $c\lambda(b-a)+2$ intervals, each of length $\le2\delta/\lambda$
(since $f'$ moves at speed $\ge\lambda$); trivially estimate these ranges
by their lengths.  On the complementary intervals apply
Lemma~\ref{lem:KL}.  This gives
$|S|\ll(c\lambda(b-a)+1)\bigl(\delta/\lambda+\delta^{-1}\bigr)$;
choosing $\delta=\lambda^{1/2}$ yields the claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Weyl differencing]\label{lem:weyl}
For any $1\le H\le N$ and $f:[1,N]\to\mathbb R$,
\[
\Bigl|\sum_{l\le N}e(f(l))\Bigr|^2
\le\frac{2N}{H}\sum_{0\le h<H}
\Bigl|\sum_{l\le N-h}e\bigl(f(l+h)-f(l)\bigr)\Bigr| .
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Average the sum over $H$ shifts, apply Cauchy--Schwarz, and expand the
square; the diagonal terms give the $h$-sum.  Standard bookkeeping.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[$r$-th derivative test]\label{lem:vdcr}
Let $r\ge2$, let $\mathcal I$ be an interval containing $N\ge1$
integers, and let $f\in C^r(\mathcal I)$ satisfy
$\lambda\le|f^{(r)}|\le A\lambda$ on $\mathcal I$, $A\ge1$.  Then
\[
\Bigl|\sum_{l\in\mathcal I}e(f(l))\Bigr|
\le C_{0}\,A^{2^{2-r}}\Bigl(N\lambda^{1/(2^{r}-2)}
+N^{1-2^{2-r}}\lambda^{-1/(2^{r}-2)}\Bigr),
\]
with $C_{0}$ an absolute constant, independent of $r$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Appendix~\ref{app:regimes}, Lemma~\ref{lem:vdcrB}: the induction on $r$ via
Lemma~\ref{lem:weyl} squares the sum before invoking the inductive
hypothesis and takes a square root after, so the constants obey
$C_r\le(2+12C_{r-1})^{1/2}$ and converge to a fixed point $<13$ rather
than compounding.  The choice of the Weyl parameter is exact, by the
identity $\theta(1-2\mu_r)=2\mu_r$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Type I bound, adaptive order]\label{lem:typeI}
Let $j\ge1$ and let $g(l)=c\,n\,l^{-j}$ (amplitude $F\asymp|c|nL^{-j}$)
on a fine block of $N$ integers inside $l\sim L$.  Note
$g^{(r)}(l)=(-1)^r c\,n\,(j)_r\,l^{-j-r}$ with the Pochhammer factor
$(j)_r=j(j+1)\cdots(j+r-1)$ (not $j^r$; for $j=1$ it is $r!$).  Choose
$r=\max\bigl(2,\lceil\log F/\log N\rceil+1\bigr)$.  Then, provided
$F\ge T_0$ (a fixed power of $\log n$, \eqref{eq:T0}),
\[
\Bigl|\sum_{l}e(g(l))\Bigr|\ll N^{1-\eta'},
\qquad
\eta'=\begin{cases}
\tfrac14\log F/\log N,& r=2\ \ (F\le N),\\[2pt]
\ge2^{-r_{\max}-1},& r\ge3,
\end{cases}
\]
uniformly over all regimes arising in this paper ($r\le r_{\max}=3J+5$).
The consecutive fixed-$r$ windows overlap, so the union covers every
amplitude $T_0\le F\le HQ^{J+1}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Appendix~\ref{app:regimes}, Lemma~\ref{lem:typeIB} (which carries $(j)_r$
explicitly and verifies the window overlap, Remark~\ref{rem:window}).
At $r=2$ the saving is a power of the amplitude alone, which is what
extends the range down to $F\asymp(\log n)^{O(1)}$.
\end{proof}

\section{Vaughan decomposition}\label{sec:vaughan}

\begin{lemma}[Vaughan]\label{lem:vaughan}
Let $U\ge2$ and $Q\ge U^{2}$.  For any function
$\phi:(Q,2Q]\to\mathbb C$ with $|\phi|\le1$,
\[
\Bigl|\sum_{p\sim Q}\phi(p)\Bigr|
\;\ll\;\frac{Q}{U}+
(\log Q)^{2}\max_{M\le U^{2}}\;\sum_{m\sim M}\,
\max_{I}\Bigl|\sum_{l\in I:\ ml\sim Q}\phi(ml)\Bigr|
+(\log Q)^{2}\!\!\max_{\substack{U/2\le M\le Q/U}}\;
\bigl|\mathcal B_{M}(\phi)\bigr| ,
\]
where $\mathcal B_{M}(\phi)=\sum_{m\sim M}\sum_{l:\ ml\sim Q}
a_{m}b_{l}\,\phi(ml)$ for certain coefficients with
$|a_m|\le\tau(m)$, $|b_l|\le\log l$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Apply the identity
$\Lambda=\mu_{\le U}*\log-\mu_{\le U}*\Lambda_{\le U}*1
+\mu_{>U}*\Lambda_{>U}*1$ to
$\sum_{q\sim Q}\Lambda(q)\phi(q)$, split all convolution ranges
dyadically, and pass from $\Lambda$-weights to primes by partial
summation (the prime powers $q=p^{i}$, $i\ge2$, contribute
$O(Q^{1/2}\log Q)$).  The first two convolutions produce the Type~I
term (the inner variable carries the smooth weight $\log$ or $1$,
removable by partial summation); the third produces the bilinear term
with the stated coefficient bounds.  The computation is carried out in
full in Appendix~\ref{app:vaughan}; see Lemma~\ref{lem:vaughanA} there
for the precise form proved.  The amendments (i) and (ii) of
Remark~\ref{rem:vaughanamend} --- the absolute value inside the $m$-sum,
and the endpoint $U/2$ --- are already incorporated in the statement
above; (iii), that in the bilinear term the inner variable likewise runs
over a subinterval, the cut being on the product $ml$, is a convention
carried through Appendix~\ref{app:regimes}.
\end{proof}

\section{The bilinear bound}\label{sec:typeII}

\begin{lemma}[Type II bound, effective form]\label{lem:typeII}
Let $j\ge1$, $\Phi(m,l)=h\,n/(ml)^{j}$ with governing amplitude
$F=|h|\,n/Q^{j}$ on $ml\asymp Q$, and let
$Q^{1/3}/2\le M\le Q^{1/2}$, $L=Q/M$, with coefficients
$|a_m|\le\tau(m)$, $|b_l|\le\log l$.  If $Fj\ge e^{80}\varrho^{10}$,
then
\[
|\mathcal B_{M}|\;\ll\;Q\,(\log Q)^{5/2}
\Bigl(L^{-1/2}+(Fj)^{-1/10}+M^{-2^{-r_{\max}-2}}\Bigr).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Appendix~\ref{app:regimes}, Lemma~\ref{lem:typeIIB}.  The structure: split
the $l$-range into $\ll\varrho$ fine blocks, Cauchy--Schwarz in $m$
(absorbing $\tau(m)^2$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:tau2}); the diagonal gives
the $L^{-1/2}$; for $l\ne l'$ the differenced phase in $m$ is a
monomial of amplitude $G\asymp F\,j\,|l-l'|/L$ (the factor $j$ from the
mean value theorem); pairs with $G$ below the threshold
$G_0=(Fj)^{4/5}$ are \emph{counted} rather than estimated, and the
remainder is estimated by Lemma~\ref{lem:typeI} in $m$.  The exponent
$4/5$ balances the two contributions.  The exchange of the roles of $m$
and $l$ needed to reach $M\le Q^{1/2}$ is Lemma~\ref{lem:typeIIsym},
which uses only $\sum|a|^2\ll M(\log2M)^3$ and $|b|_\infty\le\log Q$.
\end{proof}

\section{Scheduling: covering every regime}\label{sec:schedule}

The parameters are chosen diagonally in $n$:
\[
\varepsilon_n=(\log\log n)^{-1/3},\qquad
J(n)=\bigl\lceil(\log\log n)^{1/3}\bigr\rceil,\qquad
K(n)=(\log n)^{1-\varepsilon_n},\qquad
H(n)=(\log n)^{2} .
\]
For a band $k\le K(n)$, a fine block $(Q,\rho Q]$ inside it, and a
frequency vector with top non-vanishing level $i_0\le J(k)$, the
governing amplitude is $F=|h_{i_0}|\,n\,Q^{-(k-i_0)}$ with
$1\le|h_{i_0}|\le H$; by the exclusion of the top level in
Proposition~\ref{prop:equi}, $F\ge Q$ always, and $F\le H\,Q^{\,J+1}$.
With the adaptive order $r=\max(2,\lceil\log F/\log N\rceil+1)\le
r_{\max}=3J+5$ in Lemmas~\ref{lem:typeI} and~\ref{lem:typeII}, the
saving exponent is
\[
\eta'\gg 2^{-r_{\max}}\ge\exp\bigl(-c(\log\log n)^{1/3}\bigr),
\]
so that
\[
L^{\eta'}\ge\exp\Bigl(c'\,(\log n)^{\varepsilon_n}\,
e^{-c(\log\log n)^{1/3}}\Bigr)\;\gg\;(\log n)^{A}
\]
for every fixed $A$, uniformly over $k\le K(n)$: the block length
satisfies $\log L\asymp\log Q\ge\tfrac13\log Q\gg(\log n)^{\varepsilon_n}
=\exp\bigl((\log\log n)^{2/3}\bigr)$, which dominates
$2^{r_{\max}}=\exp\bigl(O((\log\log n)^{1/3})\bigr)$.  \emph{The full
regime verification --- that every $(F,Q,i,k)$ triple arising in the
theorem falls into the derivative-test range of Lemma~\ref{lem:typeI}
(possible since $F\ge Q\ge T_0$ throughout after the top-level
exclusion), with the residual near-stationary regime arising only in
the corroborative top-level analysis --- is set out case-by-case in
Appendix~\ref{app:regimes}.}

Combining Lemmas~\ref{lem:vaughan}--\ref{lem:typeII} with the
$J$-dimensional Erd\H{o}s--Tur\'an inequality (proved with product
Fej\'er kernels in Appendix~\ref{app:ET}; the weak form with
$(\log)$-power losses suffices) yields Proposition~\ref{prop:equi} with
$o(1)\le(\log n)^{-1}$, uniformly in $n$ and $k\le K(n)$.

\section{Assembly}\label{sec:assembly}

Assuming Proposition~\ref{prop:equi}:
\[
E(n)\;\le\;
\sum_{k\le K}\Bigl(2^{-J(k)}+o(1)\Bigr)\Bigl(\log\bigl(1+\tfrac1k\bigr)+O(\cdot)\Bigr)
\;+\;\sum_{K<k\le\log_2 n}\frac{C}{k}

\section{The Vaughan computation}\label{app:vaughan}

Throughout this appendix $U\ge2$ and $Q\ge U^{2}$ are real parameters and
$\phi:(Q,2Q]\to\mathbb C$ satisfies $|\phi|\le1$.  We write $*$ for
Dirichlet convolution, $\mathbf 1$ for the constant arithmetic function
$1$, $\delta$ for the convolution identity ($\delta(1)=1$, $\delta(q)=0$
for $q>1$), and, for a real $V>0$,
\[
\mu_{\le V}=\mu\cdot\mathbf 1_{[1,V]},\qquad \mu_{>V}=\mu-\mu_{\le V},
\qquad
\Lambda_{\le V}=\Lambda\cdot\mathbf 1_{[1,V]},\qquad
\Lambda_{>V}=\Lambda-\Lambda_{\le V}.
\]
The only facts about arithmetic functions used are
$\mu*\mathbf 1=\delta$, $\Lambda*\mathbf 1=\log$ and hence
$\mu*\log=\Lambda$; all three are Möbius inversion.

\subsection*{A.1\quad The identity}

\begin{lemma}[Vaughan's identity]\label{lem:vid}
For every integer $q>U$,
\[
\Lambda(q)=\bigl(\mu_{\le U}*\log\bigr)(q)
-\bigl(\mu_{\le U}*\Lambda_{\le U}*\mathbf 1\bigr)(q)
+\bigl(\mu_{>U}*\Lambda_{>U}*\mathbf 1\bigr)(q).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Expand the last convolution using
$\mu_{>U}=\mu-\mu_{\le U}$ and $\Lambda_{>U}=\Lambda-\Lambda_{\le U}$.
Convolution is commutative and bilinear, so
\begin{align*}
\mu_{>U}*\Lambda_{>U}*\mathbf 1
&=\mu*\Lambda*\mathbf 1-\mu*\Lambda_{\le U}*\mathbf 1
-\mu_{\le U}*\Lambda*\mathbf 1+\mu_{\le U}*\Lambda_{\le U}*\mathbf 1\\
&=\mu*\log-\Lambda_{\le U}*(\mu*\mathbf 1)
-\mu_{\le U}*\log+\mu_{\le U}*\Lambda_{\le U}*\mathbf 1\\
&=\Lambda-\Lambda_{\le U}-\mu_{\le U}*\log
+\mu_{\le U}*\Lambda_{\le U}*\mathbf 1 ,
\end{align*}
where the second line used $\Lambda*\mathbf 1=\log$ twice and the third
used $\mu*\mathbf 1=\delta$ and $\mu*\log=\Lambda$.  Rearranging,
\[
\Lambda=\mu_{\le U}*\log-\mu_{\le U}*\Lambda_{\le U}*\mathbf 1
+\mu_{>U}*\Lambda_{>U}*\mathbf 1+\Lambda_{\le U},
\]
an identity of arithmetic functions.  The final term vanishes at every
$q>U$.
\end{proof}

\subsection*{A.2\quad The divisor moment}

The Type~II proof (Lemma~\ref{lem:typeII}) consumes the following mean
value; we prove it from Mertens' theorem only.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:tau2}
For $x\ge2$, $\sum_{m\le x}\tau(m)^{2}\ll x(\log x)^{3}$.
Consequently $\sum_{m\sim M}\tau(m)^{2}\ll M(\log 2M)^{3}$ for $M\ge1$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Define $h:=\tau^{2}*\mu$, so that $\tau^{2}=h*\mathbf 1$; as a
convolution of multiplicative functions $h$ is multiplicative, and
\[
h(p^{a})=\tau(p^{a})^{2}-\tau(p^{a-1})^{2}=(a+1)^{2}-a^{2}=2a+1>0 .
\]
Hence $h\ge0$ and, exchanging the order of summation,
\[
\sum_{m\le x}\tau(m)^{2}=\sum_{d\le x}h(d)\fl{x/d}
\le x\sum_{d\le x}\frac{h(d)}{d}
\le x\prod_{p\le x}\Bigl(1+\sum_{a\ge1}\frac{2a+1}{p^{a}}\Bigr),
\]
the last step because every $d\le x$ has all its prime factors $\le x$ and
all terms are non-negative.  For $0<x_{0}\le\tfrac12$,
\[
\sum_{a\ge1}(2a+1)x_{0}^{a}
=\frac{2x_{0}}{(1-x_{0})^{2}}+\frac{x_{0}}{1-x_{0}}
=\frac{x_{0}(3-x_{0})}{(1-x_{0})^{2}} ,
\]
and multiplying out,
\[
(1-x_{0})^{3}+x_{0}(3-x_{0})(1-x_{0})
=(1-3x_{0}+3x_{0}^{2}-x_{0}^{3})+(3x_{0}-4x_{0}^{2}+x_{0}^{3})
=1-x_{0}^{2}\le1 ,
\]
so $1+x_{0}(3-x_{0})(1-x_{0})^{-2}\le(1-x_{0})^{-3}$.  Taking
$x_{0}=1/p\le\tfrac12$ gives
\[
\sum_{m\le x}\tau(m)^{2}\le x\prod_{p\le x}\Bigl(1-\frac1p\Bigr)^{-3}.
\]
Finally $\log\prod_{p\le x}(1-1/p)^{-1}
=\sum_{p\le x}\bigl(1/p+O(1/p^{2})\bigr)=\log\log x+O(1)$ by Mertens'
theorem $\sum_{p\le x}1/p=\log\log x+M+O(1/\log x)$ (elementary; the
finite form used elsewhere in this paper is machine-verified), whence
$\prod_{p\le x}(1-1/p)^{-3}\ll(\log x)^{3}$.  The dyadic form follows by
$\sum_{m\sim M}\tau(m)^{2}\le\sum_{m\le 2M}\tau(m)^{2}$, the case
$M<2$ being trivial.
\end{proof}

\subsection*{A.3\quad The three sums}

For $Q<T\le2Q$ set
\[
S(T):=\sum_{Q<q\le T}\Lambda(q)\phi(q).
\]
Every $q$ occurring satisfies $q>Q\ge U^{2}\ge U$, so Lemma~\ref{lem:vid}
applies termwise and $S(T)=\Sigma_{1}(T)-\Sigma_{2}(T)+\Sigma_{3}(T)$
with
\begin{align}
\Sigma_{1}(T)&=\sum_{d\le U}\mu(d)\!\!\sum_{l:\ Q<dl\le T}\!\!
\phi(dl)\,\log l ,\label{eq:S1}\\
\Sigma_{2}(T)&=\sum_{m}c_{m}\!\!\sum_{l:\ Q<ml\le T}\!\!\phi(ml),
\qquad c_{m}:=\!\!\sum_{\substack{de=m\\ d\le U,\ e\le U}}\!\!\mu(d)\Lambda(e),
\label{eq:S2}\\
\Sigma_{3}(T)&=\sum_{\substack{Q<ml\le T}}w(m)\,\Lambda_{>U}(l)\,\phi(ml),
\qquad w(m):=\sum_{\substack{d\mid m,\ d>U}}\mu(d).\label{eq:S3}
\end{align}
Here \eqref{eq:S1} is the convolution $\mu_{\le U}*\log$ written out with
$q=dl$ (so that the inner variable carries the weight $\log l$);
\eqref{eq:S2} collects the two inner variables of
$\mu_{\le U}*\Lambda_{\le U}*\mathbf 1$ into $m=de$, the third variable
becoming $l$; and \eqref{eq:S3} writes
$\mu_{>U}*\Lambda_{>U}*\mathbf 1$ with $m$ carrying $\mu_{>U}*\mathbf 1$
and $l$ carrying $\Lambda_{>U}$.

Three elementary properties of the coefficients:

\begin{itemize}
\item $c_{m}$ is supported on $m\le U^{2}$ and
$|c_{m}|\le\sum_{de=m}\Lambda(e)=\log m\le2\log U\le\log Q$.
\item $|w(m)|\le\sum_{d\mid m}1=\tau(m)$, and $w$ is supported on $m>U$:
for $m\le U$ every divisor of $m$ is $\le U$, so
$w(m)=\delta(m)-\sum_{d\mid m}\mu(d)=\delta(m)-\delta(m)=0$.
\item $\Lambda_{>U}(l)$ is supported on $l>U$ and
$0\le\Lambda_{>U}(l)\le\log l$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{A.4\quad Removing the smooth weight from $\Sigma_{1}$}

Only $\Sigma_{1}$ carries a weight depending on the inner variable.  Fix
$d\le U$ and put $A(u):=\sum_{Q/d<l\le u}\phi(dl)$ for
$u\in[Q/d,T/d]$.  Partial summation (Riemann--Stieltjes, or the finite
Abel identity, applied to $\log$ which is $C^{1}$) gives
\[
\sum_{Q/d<l\le T/d}\phi(dl)\log l
=A(T/d)\log(T/d)-\int_{Q/d}^{T/d}A(u)\,\frac{du}{u},
\]
whence, since $\int_{Q/d}^{T/d}du/u=\log(T/Q)\le\log2$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:remove-log}
\Bigl|\sum_{Q/d<l\le T/d}\phi(dl)\log l\Bigr|
\le\bigl(\log(2Q)+\log 2\bigr)\sup_{u}|A(u)|
\le2\log(2Q)\max_{I}\Bigl|\sum_{l\in I}\phi(dl)\Bigr| ,
\end{equation}
the maximum being over subintervals $I\subseteq(Q/d,T/d]$.  (Only
$A$ itself, not $|A|$, occurs, so no divisor-type loss is incurred.)

It is this appearance of a maximum over subintervals --- forced by the
removal of $\log l$, and independently by the cutoff at $T$ --- that
makes the Type~I object below an inner sum over an \emph{interval} rather
than over a full dyadic block; the van der Corput estimates of
\S\ref{sec:typeI} are stated on an arbitrary $[a,b]$ and are insensitive
to this.

\subsection*{A.5\quad Dyadic splitting}

Define, for $M\ge1$ and $Q<T\le 2Q$,
\[
\mathcal T_{M}(T):=\sum_{m\sim M}\ \max_{I\subseteq(Q/m,\,T/m]}
\Bigl|\sum_{l\in I}\phi(ml)\Bigr| ,
\]
\[
\mathcal B_{M}(T):=\max_{I}\Bigl|\sum_{m\sim M}\sum_{l\in I_{m}}
a_{m}b_{l}\,\phi(ml)\Bigr| ,
\]
where in $\mathcal B_{M}$ the coefficients satisfy $|a_{m}|\le\tau(m)$,
$|b_{l}|\le\log l$, and the inner range is $I_{m}=\{l: Q<ml\le T'\}$ for
a single $T'\in(Q,T]$ (i.e.\ the constraint is a cut on the product
$ml$, uniform in $m$).

\emph{$\Sigma_{1}$.}  Split $d\le U$ into the $\le\log_{2}U+1$ dyadic
ranges $d\sim D$, $D=U2^{-i}$, $0\le i\le\log_{2}U$.  By $|\mu|\le1$ and
\eqref{eq:remove-log},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:S1bd}
|\Sigma_{1}(T)|\le2\log(2Q)\,(\log_{2}U+1)\max_{D\le U}\mathcal T_{D}(T)
\ll(\log Q)^{2}\max_{M\le U^{2}}\mathcal T_{M}(T).
\end{equation}

\emph{$\Sigma_{2}$.}  Split $m\le U^{2}$ dyadically into
$\le\log_{2}(U^{2})+1$ ranges and use $|c_{m}|\le\log Q$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:S2bd}
|\Sigma_{2}(T)|\le\log Q\,\bigl(2\log_{2}U+1\bigr)
\max_{M\le U^{2}}\mathcal T_{M}(T)
\ll(\log Q)^{2}\max_{M\le U^{2}}\mathcal T_{M}(T).
\end{equation}

\emph{$\Sigma_{3}$.}  Here $m>U$ and $l>U$; since $ml\le T\le 2Q$ we get
$m<2Q/U$.  Cover $(U,2Q/U]$ by the blocks $(M_{i},2M_{i}]$ with
$M_{i}=(Q/U)2^{-i}$, $0\le i\le I:=\lceil\log_{2}(Q/U^{2})\rceil$; the
top block reaches $2Q/U$ and the bottom one has
$M_{I}\in[U/2,U]$, so all $M_{i}$ lie in $[U/2,\,Q/U]$ and
$I+1\ll\log Q$.  With $a_{m}:=w(m)$ ($|a_{m}|\le\tau(m)$) and
$b_{l}:=\Lambda_{>U}(l)$ ($|b_{l}|\le\log l$),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:S3bd}
|\Sigma_{3}(T)|\ll\log Q\max_{U/2\le M\le Q/U}\mathcal B_{M}(T).
\end{equation}

\subsection*{A.6\quad Prime powers and passage to primes}

Let $W(T):=\sum_{Q<p\le T}\phi(p)\log p$.  Then
$S(T)-W(T)=\sum_{Q<p^{i}\le T,\,i\ge2}\phi(p^{i})\log p$, and
\[
\Bigl|\sum_{\substack{Q<p^{i}\le2Q\\ i\ge2}}\phi(p^{i})\log p\Bigr|
\le\sum_{2\le i\le\log_{2}(2Q)}\ \sum_{p\le(2Q)^{1/i}}\log p
\ll Q^{1/2}+Q^{1/3}\log Q\ll Q^{1/2}\log Q ,
\]
using only Chebyshev's bound $\sum_{p\le y}\log p\ll y$ (elementary) and
the fact that there are $\ll\log Q$ values of $i$.  Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pp}
\sup_{Q<T\le2Q}|W(T)|\le\sup_{Q<T\le2Q}|S(T)|+O\bigl(Q^{1/2}\log Q\bigr).
\end{equation}
Removing the weight $\log p$ by partial summation,
\[
\sum_{p\sim Q}\phi(p)=\frac{W(2Q)}{\log 2Q}
+\int_{Q}^{2Q}\frac{W(T)}{T\log^{2}T}\,dT ,
\]
so that, since $\int_{Q}^{2Q}dT/(T\log^{2}T)\le\log2/\log^{2}Q$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:toprimes}
\Bigl|\sum_{p\sim Q}\phi(p)\Bigr|
\le\frac{2}{\log Q}\sup_{Q<T\le2Q}|W(T)| .
\end{equation}

\subsection*{A.7\quad Conclusion}

\begin{lemma}[Vaughan, appendix form]\label{lem:vaughanA}
Let $U\ge2$, $Q\ge U^{2}$, and $\phi:(Q,2Q]\to\mathbb C$ with
$|\phi|\le1$.  Then
\[
\Bigl|\sum_{p\sim Q}\phi(p)\Bigr|
\ \ll\ \frac{Q}{U}
+(\log Q)^{2}\max_{M\le U^{2}}\ \sup_{Q<T\le2Q}\mathcal T_{M}(T)
+(\log Q)^{2}\max_{U/2\le M\le Q/U}\ \sup_{Q<T\le2Q}\mathcal B_{M}(T),
\]
with $\mathcal T_{M},\mathcal B_{M}$ as in \S A.5 and coefficient bounds
$|a_{m}|\le\tau(m)$, $|b_{l}|\le\log l$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Combine \eqref{eq:S1bd}, \eqref{eq:S2bd}, \eqref{eq:S3bd} into
$|S(T)|\ll(\log Q)^{2}\max_{M\le U^{2}}\mathcal T_{M}(T)
+(\log Q)\max_{M}\mathcal B_{M}(T)$, insert into \eqref{eq:pp} and
\eqref{eq:toprimes}, and divide by $\log Q$ (which only helps).  The
prime-power term contributes $O(Q^{1/2})$, and $Q^{1/2}\le Q/U$ because
$U\le Q^{1/2}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Amendments to Lemma~\ref{lem:vaughan}]\label{rem:vaughanamend}
Two features of Lemma~\ref{lem:vaughanA} differ from the statement of
Lemma~\ref{lem:vaughan} in the main text, and
Lemma~\ref{lem:vaughan} should be read as amended.

\emph{(i) The Type~I term carries the absolute value inside the
$m$-sum.}  Lemma~\ref{lem:vaughan} displays
$\bigl|\sum_{m\sim M}\sum_{l}\phi(ml)\bigr|$; the computation above
delivers $\sum_{m\sim M}\bigl|\sum_{l}\phi(ml)\bigr|$.  The two differ
essentially: the coefficients $\mu(d)$ in \eqref{eq:S1} and $c_{m}$ in
\eqref{eq:S2} depend on $m$ and are not of constant sign, so they cannot
be removed from inside the $m$-sum by partial summation, only pulled out
in absolute value at the cost of the factor $\log Q$ already present.
The amended form is the one the paper uses: item~(I) of
\S\ref{sec:equi} already writes the Type~I family as
$\sum_{m\sim M}\bigl|\sum_{l\sim L}e(\cdot)\bigr|$.

\emph{(ii) Ranges.}  The Type~I range $M\le U^{2}$ is exactly as stated.
The bilinear range is $U/2\le M\le Q/U$ rather than $U\le M\le Q/U$: with
$m\sim M$ meaning $M<m\le2M$, a dyadic ladder inside $(U,2Q/U]$ cannot
have all its base points $\ge U$ and simultaneously reach $2Q/U$.  The
half is harmless --- Lemma~\ref{lem:typeII} is applied in
Appendix~\ref{app:regimes} only for $M$ exceeding a fixed power of
$\log Q$.

\emph{(iii) Inner range.}  In both terms the inner variable runs over a
subinterval, not a full dyadic block; see \S A.4.
\end{remark}

as in \S\ref{sec:schedule}.  Fix a band index $k\le K$ and put

P:=n^{1/k},\qquad P_{0}:=n^{1/(k+1)},\qquad
\text{band}=(P_{0},P].
\]
For $p$ in the band write $t=t(p):=n/p^{k}$; then $t$ decreases from
$P_{0}$ (at $p=P_{0}$, since $n/P_{0}^{k}=P_{0}^{k+1-k}=P_{0}$) to $1$
(at $p=P$), so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:trange}
1\le t\le P_{0}\le Q\qquad\text{for every }p\text{ in the band.}
\end{equation}
Levels are indexed by $i$ with exponent $j_{i}:=k-i+1$; the level-$i$
coordinate is $\{n/p^{j_{i}}\}$.  We set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Jk}
J(k):=\min\{J,k-1\},
\end{equation}
so that $j_{i}\ge1$ for every $i\le J(k)+1$ --- the level set
$i=2,\dots,J(k)+1$ being the operative one, by
Remark~\ref{rem:bridge}.  \textbf{[A1]}
Proposition~\ref{prop:equi} and \S\ref{sec:schedule} must be read with
$J(k)$ in place of $J$: for $k<J$ the band has only $k$ levels and the
coordinate $\{n/p^{k-i+1}\}$ is undefined for $i>k$.  (This costs
nothing: in \S\ref{sec:assembly} the terms $k<J$ contribute
$\sum_{k<J}2^{-k}/k=O(1)$.)  The hypothesis ``$J\le c\log k$'' of
Proposition~\ref{prop:equi} is likewise inconsistent with the schedule
$J=\lceil(\log\log n)^{1/3}\rceil$ of \S\ref{sec:schedule} and with
$J(k)=\fl{c\log k}$ of the remark following
Remark~\ref{rem:noshortcut}; see \textbf{[A6]}.

Finally set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rmax}
r_{\max}:=3J+5,\qquad \varrho:=k+r_{\max}=k+3J+5,\qquad
\rho:=1+\frac1\varrho .
\end{equation}

For a frequency vector
$\mathbf h=(h_{1},\dots,h_{J(k)+1})\in\mathbb Z^{J(k)+1}$
with $0<\|\mathbf h\|_{\infty}\le H$ the phase is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:phase}
\Psi_{\mathbf h}(x)=\sum_{i\le J(k)+1}h_{i}\,n\,x^{-j_{i}},
\end{equation}
and $S(\mathbf h)=\sum_{p\in\text{band}}e(\Psi_{\mathbf h}(p))$ is the sum
of \eqref{eq:weyl}.  We write
\begin{equation}\label{eq:i0}
i_{0}=i_{0}(\mathbf h):=\max\{i:h_{i}\ne0\},\qquad
j:=j_{i_{0}}=k-i_{0}+1,\qquad
F:=|h_{i_{0}}|\,n\,Q^{-j},
\end{equation}
$Q$ being the left endpoint of the block under consideration.  By
\eqref{eq:trange},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Frange}
F=|h_{i_{0}}|\,t\,Q^{\,i_{0}-1},\qquad\text{so}\qquad
1\le F\le H\,Q^{\,J+1},\qquad\text{and}\qquad
F\ge Q\ \text{ whenever }i_{0}\ge2 .
\end{equation}
\textbf{[A2]}  \S\ref{sec:schedule} speaks of ``the amplitude
$F_{i}=hn/Q^{k-i+1}$'' as though each level contributed its own regime.
The phase \eqref{eq:phase} is a \emph{sum} of monomials, and what governs
every derivative test is the single amplitude $F$ of the \emph{highest}
non-vanishing level, \eqref{eq:i0}; Lemma~\ref{lem:dominance} below shows
that the lower levels are dominated by a factor $Q^{-1}e^{r}$ and never
interfere.  Read this way the sentence is correct, and the range
$F\in[h,hQ^{i}]$ displayed there is \eqref{eq:Frange}.

\subsection*{B.1\quad The $r$-th derivative test with a constant uniform in $r$}

Lemma~\ref{lem:vdcr} as displayed carries $N^{r-2}$ inside the first
term.  That statement is true --- it is implied by the one below, since
$N\ge1$ --- but it is too weak to support the ``in particular'' clause of
Lemma~\ref{lem:typeI}: with $\lambda N^{r-2}=Fj^{r}/L^{2}$ and
$F\le L^{r-\eta}$ the first term is $\ge L$ as soon as $r\ge3$, so no
saving results.  \textbf{[A3]}  The correct first term is
$N\lambda^{1/(2^{r}-2)}$, and Lemma~\ref{lem:vdcr} should be restated
as follows.

\begin{lemma}[$r$-th derivative test, sharp form]\label{lem:vdcrB}
Let $r\ge2$, let $\mathcal I$ be an interval containing $N\ge1$ integers,
and let $f\in C^{r}(\mathcal I)$ satisfy
$\lambda\le|f^{(r)}|\le A\lambda$ on $\mathcal I$ with $\lambda>0$,
$A\ge1$.  Then
\[
\Bigl|\sum_{l\in\mathcal I}e(f(l))\Bigr|
\le C_{0}\,A^{2^{2-r}}\Bigl(N\lambda^{1/(2^{r}-2)}
+N^{1-2^{2-r}}\lambda^{-1/(2^{r}-2)}\Bigr),
\]
where $C_{0}$ is an absolute constant --- in particular independent
of $r$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $\mu_{r}=1/(2^{r}-2)$, $\beta_{r}=2^{2-r}$, and
$B_{r}(N,\lambda):=N\lambda^{\mu_{r}}+N^{1-\beta_{r}}\lambda^{-\mu_{r}}$.
Since $\lambda\le|f^{(r)}|$ and $f^{(r)}$ is continuous, $f^{(r)}$ has
constant sign on $\mathcal I$.

If $N\le2$ the claim is trivial: the left side is $\le2$, while
$B_{r}\ge2\sqrt{N\cdot N^{1-\beta_{r}}}=2N^{1-\beta_{r}/2}\ge2$ by
AM--GM, so $C_{0}\ge1$ suffices.  Assume $N\ge2$.

\emph{Case $r=2$.}  This is Lemma~\ref{lem:vdc2} with $c=A$; the proof
given there yields $|S|\le c_{2}A(N\lambda^{1/2}+\lambda^{-1/2})$ with
$c_{2}$ absolute, and $A^{2^{2-2}}=A$, $\mu_{2}=1/2$, $\beta_{2}=1$.

\emph{Case $r\ge3$, granting the statement for $r-1$.}  Two degenerate
ranges first.  If $\lambda\ge1$ then $|S|\le N\le N\lambda^{\mu_{r}}$.  If
$\lambda^{-2\mu_{r}}\ge N$, then
$\lambda\le N^{-1/(2\mu_{r})}=N^{-(2^{r-1}-1)}\le N^{-(4-2^{3-r})}
=N^{-\beta_{r}/\mu_{r}}$
(the middle inequality because $2^{r-1}-1\ge4-2^{3-r}$ for $r\ge3$, with
equality at $r=3$), whence
$N^{1-\beta_{r}}\lambda^{-\mu_{r}}\ge N$ and again $|S|\le N$ suffices.

So assume $1\le\lambda^{-2\mu_{r}}<N$ and set
$\mathcal H:=\lceil\lambda^{-2\mu_{r}}\rceil$, so $1\le\mathcal H\le N$ and
$\lambda^{-2\mu_{r}}\le\mathcal H\le2\lambda^{-2\mu_{r}}$.  Apply
Lemma~\ref{lem:weyl} with $H=\mathcal H$.  The term $h=0$ contributes
$(2N/\mathcal H)\cdot N$.  For $1\le h<\mathcal H$ put
$f_{h}(x)=f(x+h)-f(x)$; by the mean value theorem
$f_{h}^{(r-1)}(x)=h\,f^{(r)}(\xi)$ for some $\xi\in(x,x+h)$, so
$h\lambda\le|f_{h}^{(r-1)}|\le Ah\lambda$.  The inductive hypothesis
(with $\theta:=\mu_{r-1}$, $\beta_{r-1}=2\beta_{r}$) gives
\[
\Bigl|\sum_{l}e(f_{h}(l))\Bigr|
\le C_{0}A^{2\beta_{r}}\Bigl(N(h\lambda)^{\theta}
+N^{1-2\beta_{r}}(h\lambda)^{-\theta}\Bigr).
\]
Since $r\ge3$ we have $\theta=1/(2^{r-1}-2)\le\tfrac12$, so
$\sum_{1\le h<\mathcal H}h^{\theta}\le2\mathcal H^{1+\theta}$ and
$\sum_{1\le h<\mathcal H}h^{-\theta}\le2\mathcal H^{1-\theta}$.  Therefore
\[
|S|^{2}\le\frac{2N^{2}}{\mathcal H}
+4C_{0}A^{2\beta_{r}}\Bigl(N^{2}(\lambda\mathcal H)^{\theta}
+N^{2-2\beta_{r}}(\lambda\mathcal H)^{-\theta}\Bigr).
\]
Now the arithmetic identity that makes the choice of $\mathcal H$ exact:
\[
\theta\,(1-2\mu_{r})
=\frac{1}{2^{r-1}-2}\cdot\frac{2^{r}-4}{2^{r}-2}
=\frac{1}{2^{r-1}-2}\cdot\frac{2(2^{r-1}-2)}{2^{r}-2}
=\frac{2}{2^{r}-2}=2\mu_{r}.
\]
With $\lambda\mathcal H\in[\lambda^{1-2\mu_{r}},2\lambda^{1-2\mu_{r}}]$
this gives $(\lambda\mathcal H)^{\theta}\le2\lambda^{2\mu_{r}}$ and
$(\lambda\mathcal H)^{-\theta}\le\lambda^{-2\mu_{r}}$, while
$2N^{2}/\mathcal H\le2N^{2}\lambda^{2\mu_{r}}$.  Since
$N^{2-2\beta_{r}}=(N^{1-\beta_{r}})^{2}$,
\[
|S|^{2}\le(2+12C_{0}A^{2\beta_{r}})
\Bigl(N^{2}\lambda^{2\mu_{r}}+\bigl(N^{1-\beta_{r}}\bigr)^{2}
\lambda^{-2\mu_{r}}\Bigr)
\le\bigl(2+12C_{0}\bigr)A^{2\beta_{r}}\,B_{r}(N,\lambda)^{2},
\]
using $A\ge1$ and $u^{2}+v^{2}\le(u+v)^{2}$.  Taking square roots,
$|S|\le(2+12C_{0})^{1/2}A^{\beta_{r}}B_{r}(N,\lambda)$.  Thus the
constant obeys the recursion $C_{r}\le(2+12C_{r-1})^{1/2}$, whose map
$u\mapsto(2+12u)^{1/2}$ is increasing with unique fixed point
$u^{*}=6+\sqrt{38}<13$; hence $C_{r}\le\max(c_{2},13)=:C_{0}$ for every
$r$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Trap check 1: the constant in $r$]\label{rem:trap1}
The concern that the $r$-th derivative test carries a factor
$2^{O(r)}$, with $r$ growing in $n$, does not materialise: the induction
step \emph{squares} the sum before applying the inductive hypothesis and
then takes a square root, so the constants satisfy
$C_{r}=(2+12C_{r-1})^{1/2}$ and converge rather than compound.  The
constant is absolute.

What does compound is the \emph{ratio} $A$, through the factor
$A^{2^{2-r}}$.  This factor is harmless only if $A=O(1)$, and on a
\emph{dyadic} block $A$ is not $O(1)$: for the phase $x\mapsto n x^{-j}$
on $x\sim Q$ one has
$|f^{(r)}(2Q)|/|f^{(r)}(Q)|=2^{-(j+r)}$, so $A=2^{\,j+r}$ with $j$ as
large as $k\le K=(\log n)^{1-\varepsilon_{n}}$.  Then
$A^{2^{2-r}}=\exp\bigl(4(j+r)2^{-r}\log2\bigr)$, which for
$r\le r_{\max}=3J+5$ and $j\asymp K$ is
$\exp\bigl(\Omega\bigl(K\,2^{-3J}\bigr)\bigr)
=\exp\bigl(\Omega\bigl((\log n)^{1-\varepsilon_{n}}
e^{-O((\log\log n)^{1/3})}\bigr)\bigr)$
--- larger than any power of $\log n$, and larger than the saving.  This
is the real content of the trap, and it is why \S B.3 replaces the dyadic
block by the block of ratio $\rho=1+1/\varrho$.  \textbf{[A4]}

For completeness we also record the verification demanded of the
$2^{O(r)}$ form, which holds a fortiori: with $r\le r_{\max}=3J+5$,
$2^{O(r)}=\exp\bigl(O((\log\log n)^{1/3})\bigr)$, whereas the saving
factor established in \S B.7 is
$\exp\bigl(c\,(\log n)^{\varepsilon_{n}}2^{-3J-6}\bigr)
=\exp\bigl(\exp\bigl((\log\log n)^{2/3}(1+o(1))\bigr)\bigr)$; the
logarithm of the saving already exceeds the logarithm of $2^{O(r)}$ by
an exponential factor.
\end{remark}

\subsection*{B.2\quad Band phases and dominance}

For $r\ge1$ write $(j)_{r}:=j(j+1)\cdots(j+r-1)$, so that
$\frac{d^{r}}{dx^{r}}x^{-j}=(-1)^{r}(j)_{r}x^{-j-r}$.  Note $(j)_{r}$,
not $j^{r}$, is the exact factor; the two agree only up to
$e^{O(r^{2}/j)}$, and for $j=1$ one has $(1)_{r}=r!$.  \textbf{[A5]}
Lemma~\ref{lem:typeI} writes $g^{(r)}(l)\asymp_{j,r}Fj^{r}/L^{r}$ and
then carries $j^{r}$ through the displayed bound; the correct factor is
$(j)_{r}$.  Since Lemma~\ref{lem:typeI} declares its implied constant to
depend on $j$ and $r$ the statement is not false, but $j$ and $r$ both
grow with $n$ here, so the dependence must be made explicit; \S B.4 does
this.

\begin{lemma}[Dominance]\label{lem:dominance}
Let $\mathbf h$, $i_{0}$, $j$, $F$ be as in \eqref{eq:i0}, let $2\le r\le
r_{\max}$, and let $\mathcal Q=(Q,\rho Q]$ with $Q\ge8e\,H\,e^{r_{\max}}$.
Then for all $x\in\mathcal Q$,
\[
\frac{3}{4e}\,\frac{F\,(j)_{r}}{Q^{r}}
\ \le\ \bigl|\Psi_{\mathbf h}^{(r)}(x)\bigr|
\ \le\ \frac{5}{4}\,\frac{F\,(j)_{r}}{Q^{r}} .
\]
In particular $\Psi_{\mathbf h}^{(r)}$ has constant sign on $\mathcal Q$ and
$\lambda\le|\Psi_{\mathbf h}^{(r)}|\le A\lambda$ there with
$\lambda:=\frac{3}{4e}F(j)_{r}Q^{-r}$ and $A\le\frac{5e}{3}<5$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By \eqref{eq:phase},
$\Psi_{\mathbf h}^{(r)}(x)=(-1)^{r}\sum_{i\le i_{0}}h_{i}n(j_{i})_{r}
x^{-j_{i}-r}$.  Write $x^{-j_{i}-r}=Q^{-j_{i}-r}\theta_{i}(x)$ with
$\theta_{i}(x)=(x/Q)^{-j_{i}-r}$.  Since $1\le x/Q\le\rho$ and
$j_{i}+r\le k+r_{\max}=\varrho$,
\[
\rho^{-\varrho}=\Bigl(1+\tfrac1\varrho\Bigr)^{-\varrho}\ge e^{-1}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \theta_{i}(x)\in[e^{-1},1].
\]
Put $G_{i}:=|h_{i}|\,n\,(j_{i})_{r}Q^{-j_{i}}$, so
$G_{i_{0}}=F(j)_{r}$.  For $i<i_{0}$ we have $j_{i}>j$ and
\[
\frac{(j_{i})_{r}}{(j)_{r}}=\prod_{s=0}^{r-1}\frac{j_{i}+s}{j+s}
\le\Bigl(\frac{j_{i}}{j}\Bigr)^{r}
=\Bigl(1+\frac{i_{0}-i}{j}\Bigr)^{r}\le e^{\,r(i_{0}-i)/j}
\le e^{\,r(i_{0}-i)},
\]
using that $s\mapsto(a+s)/(b+s)$ decreases for $a\ge b>0$.  Also
$|h_{i}|n Q^{-j_{i}}=|h_{i}|\,t\,Q^{i-1}\le H\,t\,Q^{i-1}$ and
$|h_{i_{0}}|nQ^{-j}=|h_{i_{0}}|tQ^{i_{0}-1}\ge tQ^{i_{0}-1}$, so
\[
\sum_{i<i_{0}}\frac{G_{i}}{G_{i_{0}}}
\le H\sum_{s\ge1}\Bigl(\frac{e^{r}}{Q}\Bigr)^{s}
\le\frac{2He^{r}}{Q}\le\frac{1}{4e},
\]
the last step by the hypothesis $Q\ge8eHe^{r_{\max}}$ (which also gives
$e^{r}\le Q/2$, justifying the geometric sum).  Consequently
\[
\bigl|\Psi^{(r)}_{\mathbf h}(x)\bigr|Q^{r}
\ \ge\ e^{-1}G_{i_{0}}-\sum_{i<i_{0}}G_{i}
\ \ge\ \Bigl(e^{-1}-\tfrac{1}{4e}\Bigr)G_{i_{0}}
=\tfrac{3}{4e}F(j)_{r},
\]
and $\le(1+\tfrac{1}{4e})G_{i_{0}}\le\tfrac54F(j)_{r}$.
\end{proof}

The hypothesis $Q\ge8eHe^{r_{\max}}$ is satisfied for every block of every
band with $k\le K$: $\log\bigl(8eHe^{r_{\max}}\bigr)
=r_{\max}+2\log\log n+O(1)=O(\log\log n)$, whereas
\begin{equation}\label{eq:logQ}
\log Q\ \ge\ \log P_{0}=\frac{\log n}{k+1}\ \ge\
\frac{\log n}{(\log n)^{1-\varepsilon_{n}}+1}\ \ge\
\tfrac12(\log n)^{\varepsilon_{n}}
=\tfrac12\exp\bigl((\log\log n)^{2/3}\bigr).
\end{equation}

\subsection*{B.3\quad Fine blocks}

\begin{definition}
A \emph{fine block} of the band is an interval $\mathcal Q=(Q,\rho Q]$
contained in $(P_{0},P]$, with $\rho=1+1/\varrho$ as in \eqref{eq:rmax}.
\end{definition}

The band is covered by
\[
\mathcal N_{\mathrm{bl}}
=\Bigl\lceil\frac{\log(P/P_{0})}{\log\rho}\Bigr\rceil
\ \le\ 1+2\varrho\,\frac{\log n}{k(k+1)}
\]
fine blocks (using $\log\rho\ge\frac{1}{2\varrho}$), and every fine block
contains $\asymp Q/\varrho$ integers.  All estimates below are proved for
one fine block and are \emph{proportional to the block length}, so
summing over blocks reproduces the band with no loss:
if $|S_{\mathcal Q}(\mathbf h)|\le|\mathcal Q|\,\Xi$ for every fine block
with a saving factor $\Xi$ independent of the block, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:blocksum}
|S(\mathbf h)|\le\sum_{\mathcal Q}|\mathcal Q|\,\Xi\le P\,\Xi .
\end{equation}
Since $P\le\pi(P)\log P$, a saving
$\Xi\ll(\log n)^{-B-1}$ gives
$|S(\mathbf h)|\ll\pi(P)\log P\,(\log n)^{-B-1}\ll\pi(P)(\log n)^{-B}$,
which is \eqref{eq:weyl}.  The saving is measured against $\log n$ and
not $\log Q$ or $\log P$; \S B.8 supplies it in that form, and \S B.9
shows why the weaker $\log P$-form would not close the argument
(\textbf{[B2]}).

\subsection*{B.4\quad Type I on a fine block: the adaptive choice of $r$}

\begin{lemma}[Type I, effective form]\label{lem:typeIB}
Let $\mathcal Q=(Q,\rho Q]$ be a fine block, $m\ge1$, and let
$\mathcal L$ be an interval of $l$ with $ml\in\mathcal Q$, containing
$N\ge2$ integers; put $L:=Q/m$.  Let $\mathbf h$, $j$, $F$ be as in
\eqref{eq:i0} and let $g(l):=\Psi_{\mathbf h}(ml)$.  Put
\[
x:=\frac{\log F}{\log N},\qquad
r:=\max\bigl(2,\lceil x\rceil+1\bigr),\qquad
\varsigma:=\frac{r\log\varrho+8r}{\log N}.
\]
Assume $2\le r\le r_{\max}$ and $\varsigma\le\min\bigl(\tfrac1{16},x/4\bigr)$
--- the latter holds as soon as $F\ge e^{64}\varrho^{8}$.  Then
\[
\Bigl|\sum_{l\in\mathcal L}e(g(l))\Bigr|\ \ll\ N^{\,1-\eta'},
\qquad
\eta':=
\begin{cases}
x/4, & r=2,\\[4pt]
\dfrac{1}{2(2^{r}-2)}\ \ge\ 2^{-r_{\max}-1}, & r\ge3,
\end{cases}
\]
with an absolute implied constant.  For $r=2$ the conclusion reads
\[
\Bigl|\sum_{l\in\mathcal L}e(g(l))\Bigr|\ \ll\ N\,F^{-1/4}:
\]
the saving is a power of the amplitude alone, independent of $N$.  This
is the point that determines where the nearly stationary regime really
begins; see \S B.6.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Since $ml\in\mathcal Q$ and $m$ is fixed, $\mathcal L\subseteq(L,\rho L]$,
and $g(l)=\sum_{i}(h_{i}nm^{-j_{i}})l^{-j_{i}}$ is a phase of the shape
\eqref{eq:phase} with $n$ replaced by $nm^{-j_{i}}$ at level $i$ and $Q$
replaced by $L$.  The amplitude of the top level is unchanged:
$|h_{i_{0}}|nm^{-j}L^{-j}=|h_{i_{0}}|n(mL)^{-j}=|h_{i_{0}}|nQ^{-j}=F$;
likewise every ratio $G_{i}/G_{i_{0}}$ in the proof of
Lemma~\ref{lem:dominance} is unchanged, because $m^{-j_{i}}L^{-j_{i}}
=Q^{-j_{i}}$ for every $i$.  Hence Lemma~\ref{lem:dominance} applies
verbatim on $\mathcal L$ and gives
\[
\lambda\le|g^{(r)}|\le A\lambda\ \text{ on }\mathcal L,\qquad
\lambda=\tfrac{3}{4e}F(j)_{r}L^{-r},\qquad A<5 .
\]
Set $\kappa:=\lambda N^{r}$.  Since $\mathcal L\subseteq(L,\rho L]$ we
have $N\le L/\varrho+1\le2L/\varrho$ and $N\ge1$, so
$(N/L)^{r}\in[\,L^{-r},\,(2/\varrho)^{r}]$, and since
$1\le(j)_{r}\le(k+r)^{r}\le\varrho^{r}$,
\[
\kappa=\tfrac{3}{4e}F\,(j)_{r}\,(N/L)^{r}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\bigl|\log\kappa-\log F\bigr|\le r\log\varrho+8r,
\quad\text{i.e.}\quad
\bigl|\log_{N}\kappa-x\bigr|\le\varsigma\le\tfrac1{16}.
\]
Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:vdcrB}.  With $\mu_{r}=1/(2^{r}-2)$ and
$\eta'=\mu_{r}/2$ the two terms are $\le N^{1-\eta'}$ provided
\begin{align}
N\lambda^{\mu_{r}}\le N^{1-\eta'}
&\iff \kappa\le N^{\,r-\eta'/\mu_{r}}=N^{\,r-1/2},
\label{eq:condU}\\
N^{1-2^{2-r}}\lambda^{-\mu_{r}}\le N^{1-\eta'}
&\iff \kappa\ \ge\ N^{\,r-(2^{2-r}-\eta')/\mu_{r}}
=N^{\,r-4+2^{3-r}+1/2}. \label{eq:condL}
\end{align}
By the displayed slack it suffices that
$x\le r-\tfrac12-\varsigma$ and $x\ge r-\tfrac72+2^{3-r}+\varsigma$.

If $r=2$ (so $x\le1$), take $\eta':=x/4$; then \eqref{eq:condU} and
\eqref{eq:condL} read $\kappa\le N^{2-x/2}$ and $\kappa\ge N^{x/2}$, both
implied by $|\log_{N}\kappa-x|\le\varsigma\le x/4$ together with $x\le1$.
Here $N^{1-\eta'}=N\,N^{-x/4}=N\,F^{-1/4}$, which is the second displayed
form.

If $r\ge3$, then $r=\lceil x\rceil+1$ gives $x\le r-1$ and $x\ge r-2$.
The upper condition needs $x\le r-\tfrac12-\varsigma$: true since
$x\le r-1$.  The lower condition needs
$x\ge r-\tfrac72+2^{3-r}+\varsigma$; for $r=3$ this is
$x\ge-\tfrac12+1+\varsigma=\tfrac12+\varsigma$, and $x\ge r-2=1$; for
$r\ge4$, $2^{3-r}\le\tfrac12$ so it is $x\ge r-3+\varsigma$, and
$x\ge r-2$.  Both hold.  Finally $\eta'=\mu_{r}/2\ge2^{-r_{\max}-1}$ for
$r\le r_{\max}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The window structure]\label{rem:window}
The mechanism is worth stating separately, since it is what the ``in
particular'' clause of Lemma~\ref{lem:typeI} was reaching for.  For fixed
$r$ the derivative test saves exactly on the window
\[
\kappa\in\bigl[N^{\,r-4+2^{3-r}+\frac12},\;N^{\,r-\frac12}\bigr],
\]
of width $3.5-2^{3-r}$ in the exponent.  Consecutive windows overlap
($r$ and $r+1$ share an exponent range of length $\ge2.5$), and their
union over $2\le r\le r_{\max}$ is
$[N^{\eta},N^{\,r_{\max}-1/2}]$ for any fixed $\eta>0$ and $n$ large.
\textbf{[A3, continued]}  Lemma~\ref{lem:typeI} asserts a saving on
$L^{\eta}\le F\le L^{r-\eta}$ \emph{for a fixed $r$}; that is false for
$r\ge5$, where the lower edge of the window is at $F\asymp L^{r-3.5}$,
not $L^{\eta}$.  The correct statement is: $r$ must be chosen as a
function of $F$ and $L$, namely $r=\max(2,\lceil\log F/\log L\rceil+1)$,
and then the saving exponent is $\eta'\asymp2^{-r}$.  The paper's
$r=J+3$ is the \emph{maximum} $r$ needed, not a fixed choice; see
\textbf{[A6]}.
\end{remark}

\subsection*{B.5\quad Type II on a fine block: the differenced amplitude}

\begin{lemma}[Trap check 2: the differenced amplitude]\label{lem:diffamp}
Let $j\ge1$, let $\mathcal L\subseteq(L,\rho L]$ with
$\rho=1+1/\varrho$, $\varrho\ge j$, and let $l,l'\in\mathcal L$,
$\Delta:=l'-l\ne0$.  Then
\[
\bigl|l^{-j}-l'^{-j}\bigr|\ \asymp\ \frac{j\,|\Delta|}{L}\,L^{-j},
\]
with absolute implied constants.  If instead $l,l'$ are allowed to range
over a full dyadic block $(L,2L]$, then
$|l^{-j}-l'^{-j}|\asymp\min(l,l')^{-j}\min\bigl(1,j|\Delta|/L\bigr)$,
and the two sides of the first display differ by a factor as large as
$2^{j}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By the mean value theorem $l^{-j}-l'^{-j}=j\Delta\,\xi^{-j-1}$ for some
$\xi$ between $l$ and $l'$.  On a fine block $\xi\in(L,\rho L]$ and
$\rho^{-(j+1)}\ge\rho^{-\varrho}\ge e^{-1}$, so
$\xi^{-j-1}\asymp L^{-j-1}$, giving the first claim.  On a dyadic block
write $l^{-j}-l'^{-j}=l^{-j}\bigl(1-(1+\Delta/l)^{-j}\bigr)$ with
$|\Delta/l|\le1$; the bracket is $\asymp j|\Delta|/l$ when
$j|\Delta|/l\le1$ and $\asymp1$ otherwise, while $l^{-j}$ ranges over
$[(2L)^{-j},L^{-j}]$.
\end{proof}

\textbf{[A6]}  Lemma~\ref{lem:typeII} states that the differenced phase
$h\,n\,m^{-j}(l^{-j}-l'^{-j})$ is ``a monomial in $m$ of amplitude
$\asymp F|l-l'|/L$''.  Two corrections are needed.  \emph{(a) The factor
$j$ is missing}: by Lemma~\ref{lem:diffamp} the amplitude is
$\asymp F\,j|l-l'|/L$.  \emph{(b) The estimate is a fine-block
statement}: over the dyadic $l$-range that Lemma~\ref{lem:typeII}
allows, it is false by a factor up to $2^{j}$, and $2^{j}$ can be as
large as $2^{K}$.  Both corrections are incorporated below.  A third
point: \emph{(c)} the proof applies Lemma~\ref{lem:typeI} \emph{in the
variable $m$}, so the saving is a power of $M$, not of $L$; the displayed
conclusion $Q(\log Q)^{3}(M^{-1/2}+L^{-\eta'/2})$ should read
$Q(\log Q)^{3}(L^{-1/2}+M^{-\eta'/2})$.  (The first summand as printed is
weaker than what the proof gives, hence harmless; the second as printed
is \emph{stronger}, hence an error.  In the application both are far
below the target.)  Finally \emph{(d)}: the hypothesis
``$L^{\eta}\le F|\Delta|/L$ for $1\le|\Delta|\le L$'' forces
$F\ge L^{1+\eta}$, which fails throughout the shallow part of every band;
Lemma~\ref{lem:typeIIB} replaces it by a count of the exceptional pairs.

\begin{lemma}[Type II, effective form]\label{lem:typeIIB}
Let $\mathcal Q=(Q,\rho Q]$ be a fine block, let $M\ge2$ with
$L:=Q/M\ge Q^{1/3}$, and let
\[
\mathcal B=\sum_{m\sim M}\ \sum_{\substack{l\,:\,ml\in\mathcal Q}}
a_{m}b_{l}\,e\bigl(\Psi_{\mathbf h}(ml)\bigr),\qquad
|a_{m}|\le\tau(m),\ |b_{l}|\le\log l .
\]
Let $\mathbf h,j,F$ be as in \eqref{eq:i0} and assume
$Fj\ge e^{80}\varrho^{10}$, and $r\le r_{\max}$ for every $r$ arising
below.  Then
\[
|\mathcal B|\ \ll\ Q(\log Q)^{5/2}
\Bigl(L^{-1/2}+(Fj)^{-1/10}+M^{-2^{-r_{\max}-2}}\Bigr),
\]
with an absolute implied constant.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The $l$'s occurring satisfy $l\in(Q/(2M),\rho Q/M]$, a range of
multiplicative width $2\rho\le3$.  Split it into
$\nu_{\max}\le1+\varrho\log3\ll\varrho$ fine blocks
$\mathcal L_{\nu}=(L_{\nu},\rho L_{\nu}]$ with $L_{\nu}\asymp L$, and write
$\mathcal B=\sum_{\nu}\mathcal B_{\nu}$ accordingly.  Fix $\nu$.  Given
$l\in\mathcal L_{\nu}$ and $ml\in\mathcal Q$, the variable $m$ lies in
$\mathcal M_{\nu}:=(Q/(\rho L_{\nu}),\rho Q/L_{\nu}]$, of multiplicative
width $\rho^{2}$; put $N_{m}:=\#(\mathcal M_{\nu}\cap\mathbb Z)\asymp
M/\varrho$.  By Cauchy--Schwarz in $m$ and Lemma~\ref{lem:tau2},
\[
|\mathcal B_{\nu}|^{2}
\le\Bigl(\sum_{m\sim M}\tau(m)^{2}\Bigr)
\sum_{m\in\mathcal M_{\nu}}\Bigl|\sum_{\substack{l\in\mathcal L_{\nu}\\
ml\in\mathcal Q}}b_{l}e(\Psi_{\mathbf h}(ml))\Bigr|^{2}
\ll M(\log 2M)^{3}\,\Sigma,
\]
where, expanding the square,
\[
\Sigma=\sum_{l,l'\in\mathcal L_{\nu}}b_{l}\overline{b_{l'}}
\sum_{m\in\mathcal M_{\nu}(l,l')}
e\bigl(\Psi_{\mathbf h}(ml)-\Psi_{\mathbf h}(ml')\bigr),
\]
and $\mathcal M_{\nu}(l,l')=\{m\in\mathcal M_{\nu}:ml,ml'\in\mathcal Q\}$
is an interval.  Since $|b_{l}|\le\log(2L)\le\log Q$, the diagonal
$l=l'$ contributes $\ll(\log Q)^{2}|\mathcal L_{\nu}|N_{m}
\ll(\log Q)^{2}Q/\varrho^{2}$.

For $l\ne l'$ the inner phase is
$\Psi_{\mathbf h}(ml)-\Psi_{\mathbf h}(ml')
=\sum_{i}h_{i}n(l^{-j_{i}}-l'^{-j_{i}})m^{-j_{i}}$, a phase of the shape
\eqref{eq:phase} in $m$ whose level-$i$ coefficient is
$h_{i}n(l^{-j_{i}}-l'^{-j_{i}})$.  By Lemma~\ref{lem:diffamp}, the
top-level amplitude at scale $M$ is
\[
G:=\bigl|h_{i_{0}}\bigr|\,n\,\bigl|l^{-j}-l'^{-j}\bigr|
\Bigl(\frac{Q}{L_{\nu}}\Bigr)^{-j}\asymp F\,\frac{j|l-l'|}{L},
\]
and the dominance estimate of Lemma~\ref{lem:dominance} holds with the
same proof (the ratios $G_{i}/G_{i_{0}}$ pick up the extra factor
$\bigl|l^{-j_{i}}-l'^{-j_{i}}\bigr|/\bigl|l^{-j}-l'^{-j}\bigr|\cdot
L^{j_{i}-j}\le j_{i}/j\le k$, absorbed by the margin
$Q\ge8eHKe^{r_{\max}}$, which \eqref{eq:logQ} again grants).

Put $G_{0}:=(Fj)^{4/5}$ and call a pair $(l,l')$ \emph{good} if
$G\ge G_{0}$.  For a good pair, Lemma~\ref{lem:typeIB} applied in the
variable $m$ (with $F$ replaced by $G$, $L$ by $M$, $N$ by $N_{m}$) gives
$|\sum_{m}e(\cdot)|\ll N_{m}\Xi$, where
\[
\Xi:=G_{0}^{-1/4}+N_{m}^{-2^{-r_{\max}-1}} :
\]
if the resulting exponent $x$ is $\le1$ the saving is
$G^{-1/4}\le G_{0}^{-1/4}$, and otherwise it is $N_{m}^{-\eta'}$ with
$\eta'\ge2^{-r_{\max}-1}$.  The hypothesis $G\ge e^{64}\varrho^{8}$ of
Lemma~\ref{lem:typeIB} holds because
$G\ge G_{0}=(Fj)^{4/5}\ge(e^{80}\varrho^{10})^{4/5}=e^{64}\varrho^{8}$.
For a bad pair we use the trivial bound $N_{m}$; by
Lemma~\ref{lem:diffamp} a pair is bad only if
$|l-l'|\ll G_{0}L/(Fj)$, so the number of bad pairs is
$\ll|\mathcal L_{\nu}|\bigl(1+G_{0}L/(Fj)\bigr)$.  Hence
\[
\Sigma\ll(\log Q)^{2}\Bigl[\frac{Q}{\varrho^{2}}
+\frac{L^{2}}{\varrho^{2}}N_{m}\Xi
+\frac{L}{\varrho}\Bigl(1+\frac{G_{0}L}{Fj}\Bigr)N_{m}\Bigr],
\]
and therefore, using $N_{m}\asymp M/\varrho$ and $ML=Q$,
\[
|\mathcal B_{\nu}|^{2}\ll M(\log Q)^{5}
\Bigl[\frac{Q}{\varrho^{2}}+\frac{Q^{2}}{M\varrho^{3}}\Xi
+\frac{Q}{\varrho^{2}}\Bigl(1+\frac{G_{0}L}{Fj}\Bigr)\Bigr].
\]
Taking square roots, summing over the $\ll\varrho$ values of $\nu$, and
using $(MQ)^{1/2}=QL^{-1/2}$,
\[
|\mathcal B|\ll(\log Q)^{5/2}\,Q\Bigl[L^{-1/2}+\Xi^{1/2}
+\Bigl(\frac{G_{0}}{Fj}\Bigr)^{1/2}\Bigr].
\]
The exponent $4/5$ is chosen to balance the last two: both
$G_{0}^{-1/8}$ and $(G_{0}/(Fj))^{1/2}$ equal $(Fj)^{-1/10}$.  Finally
$N_{m}^{-2^{-r_{\max}-2}}\le2M^{-2^{-r_{\max}-2}}$ because
$2^{-r_{\max}-2}\log\varrho=o(1)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:typeIIreach}
The bound is $\ll Q(\log Q)^{-A}$ as soon as
\[
Fj\ge(\log Q)^{10A+40}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
M\ge(\log Q)^{(A+3)2^{r_{\max}+2}},
\]
the second being automatic in
\S B.7 by \eqref{eq:logQ}.  \emph{The bilinear estimate therefore reaches
down to amplitudes that are a fixed power of $\log Q$}, not merely to
$F\ge L^{1+\eta}$ as the hypothesis of Lemma~\ref{lem:typeII} would
require, nor to $F\ge L^{\eta}$ as \S\ref{sec:schedule} suggests.  This
is what makes \S B.6 possible.
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[Symmetric range]\label{lem:typeIIsym}
Lemma~\ref{lem:typeIIB} holds verbatim with the roles of $m$ and $l$
exchanged, i.e.\ for $|a_{m}|\le\log m$, $|b_{l}|\le\tau(l)$, with $M$
and $L$ exchanged throughout.  Consequently the bilinear range
$U/2\le M\le Q/U$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:vaughanA} may be reduced to
$U/2\le M\le Q^{1/2}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:typeIIB} uses about $a$ only
$\sum_{m\sim M}|a_{m}|^{2}\ll M(\log 2M)^{3}$ and about $b$ only
$|b_{l}|\le\log Q$; both hold after the exchange, the first by
Lemma~\ref{lem:tau2} applied in $l$ and the second because
$|a_{m}|\le\log m\le\log Q$.  The phase $\Psi_{\mathbf h}(ml)$ is
symmetric in $m$ and $l$.  For the reduction: if $M>Q^{1/2}$ then
$L=Q/M<Q^{1/2}$, and applying the exchanged form to $\mathcal B$ with
outer variable $l$ gives the bound with $M$ and $L$ interchanged.
\end{proof}

\subsection*{B.6\quad Where the nearly stationary regime really begins}

By \eqref{eq:Frange}, $F\ge Q$ whenever $i_{0}\ge2$.  Small amplitudes
therefore occur only for frequency vectors supported at level one, where
$F=|h_{1}|\,t$ with $1\le t\le P_{0}$.  Fix the target saving
$(\log n)^{-B}$ of \eqref{eq:weyl} and set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:T0}
T_{0}:=e^{80}\varrho^{10}(\log n)^{10B+40}.
\end{equation}
Since $\varrho=k+r_{\max}\le3\log n$, we have
$T_{0}\le(\log n)^{10B+50}$, a fixed power of $\log n$.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{(a) $F\ge T_{0}$ is covered by the derivative tests.}
For Type~I, $F\ge T_{0}\ge e^{64}\varrho^{8}$ so Lemma~\ref{lem:typeIB}
applies; if $x\le1$ its saving is $F^{-1/4}\le(\log n)^{-(10B+40)/4}
\le(\log n)^{-B-2}$, and if $x>1$ the saving is $N^{-\eta'}$ with
$\eta'\ge2^{-r_{\max}-1}$, which is $\le(\log n)^{-B-2}$ by \S B.8.  For
Type~II, $Fj\ge F\ge e^{80}\varrho^{10}$ so Lemma~\ref{lem:typeIIB}
applies, and $(Fj)^{-1/10}\le T_{0}^{-1/10}\le(\log n)^{-B-4}$ by
\eqref{eq:T0}.

\textbf{[A8$'$]}  Hence \emph{the nearly stationary regime is
$F<T_{0}=(\log n)^{O_{B}(1)}$, not $F\le L^{\eta}$}, and the difference
is decisive rather than cosmetic.  The mechanism is the last display of
Lemma~\ref{lem:typeIB}: at $r=2$ the second-derivative test saves
$F^{-1/4}$, a power of the amplitude that does not degrade as the
interval lengthens, so a saving of a fixed power of $\log Q$ --- all this
paper asks for --- is already available at $F$ a fixed power of $\log Q$.
Were the threshold really $L^{\eta}$, the direct count of part~(b) would
require primes counted in intervals of relative length $P^{-\eta/3}$,
which is not available unconditionally; at $T_{0}$ it is routine.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{(b) $F<T_{0}$: the direct count.}  Here $i_{0}=1$ and,
since $|h_{1}|\ge1$, also $t\le F<T_{0}$.  The map $p\mapsto t=n/p^{k}$
is a decreasing bijection of the band onto $[1,P_{0}]$, and the level-one
phase $h_{1}n/p^{k}=h_{1}t$ is monotone in $p$ with total variation
$|h_{1}|T_{0}\ll F+1$ over $\{t\le T_{0}\}$.  Consequently, for any
target arc $I_{1}$, the condition $\{n/p^{k}\}\in I_{1}$ meets
$\{t\le T_{0}\}$ in $O(F+1)$ intervals of $p$ --- this is the interval
count referred to in \S\ref{sec:schedule}.  Writing
$\mathcal R_{\nu}:=\{p:t(p)\in(\nu,\nu+1]\}$ for
$\nu\le\lceil T_{0}\rceil$, each $\mathcal R_{\nu}$ is an interval of $p$
of multiplicative width $(1+1/\nu)^{1/k}$, hence of length
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Rnu}
|\mathcal R_{\nu}|\asymp\frac{P}{k\nu}\ \ge\ \frac{P}{kT_{0}}
\ \ge\ P\,(\log n)^{-10A-51}.
\end{equation}
On $\mathcal R_{\nu}$ the level-one coordinate is $\{t\}=t-\nu$, monotone
in $p$, so $\{n/p^{k}\}\in I_{1}$ cuts $\mathcal R_{\nu}$ into one
subinterval $\mathcal R_{\nu}(I_{1})$, and by Lemma~\ref{lem:measure}
(log-uniformity of the Mertens measure in the coordinate $t$)
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hyperbola}
\frac{\sum_{p\in\mathcal R_{\nu}(I_{1})}1/p}
{\sum_{p\in\mathcal R_{\nu}}1/p}
=\sigma_{\nu}(I_{1})\bigl(1+o(1)\bigr),
\qquad
\sigma_{\nu}(I_{1}):=\frac{\log\frac{\nu+\beta}{\nu+\alpha}}
{\log\frac{\nu+1}{\nu}},\quad I_{1}=[\alpha,\beta).
\end{equation}
Within $\mathcal R_{\nu}(I_{1})$ the remaining coordinates
$i=2,\dots,J(k)$ have $F\ge Q$ by \eqref{eq:Frange} and are therefore
covered by part~(a); by \eqref{eq:Rnu} that interval still has
$\log Q\asymp\log P$, so \eqref{eq:logQ} is unaffected.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{(c) What (b) costs, and the one input the paper does not
list.}  Statement \eqref{eq:hyperbola}, and the band-Mertens statement
$\sum_{p\in\text{band}}1/p=\log(1+1/k)(1+o(1))$ of component~(B), both
require the prime number theorem with the classical error term
$\pi(y)=\operatorname{li}(y)+O\bigl(y\,e^{-c\sqrt{\log y}}\bigr)$.  They
do \emph{not} follow from Chebyshev--Mertens alone: Mertens' theorem with
its elementary error gives
\[
\sum_{p\in\text{band}}\frac1p=\log\Bigl(1+\frac1k\Bigr)
+O\Bigl(\frac{k}{\log n}\Bigr),
\]
whose error exceeds the main term $\asymp1/k$ as soon as
$k>\sqrt{\log n}$, whereas the schedule runs to
$K=(\log n)^{1-\varepsilon_{n}}\gg\sqrt{\log n}$.  With the classical
error term the relative error in \eqref{eq:Rnu}--\eqref{eq:hyperbola} is
$\ll kT_{0}\,e^{-c\sqrt{\log P}}$, and by \eqref{eq:logQ}
$\sqrt{\log P}\ge(\log n)^{\varepsilon_{n}/2}
=\exp\bigl(\tfrac12(\log\log n)^{2/3}\bigr)$ while
$\log(kT_{0})=O(\log\log n)$; the margin is overwhelming.

\textbf{[A10]}  The input list of \S\ref{sec:equi} --- ``Kummer's carry
criterion \dots, the Chebyshev--Mertens bounds of \S\ref{sec:density}
(machine-verified in finite form), and the elementary analysis of
\S\S\ref{sec:vaughan}--\ref{sec:typeII}'' --- is therefore incomplete: it
must also name the prime number theorem with a classical error term.
This is an internal inconsistency rather than a new hypothesis:
Remark~\ref{rem:noshortcut} already reasons with
``PNT-with-classical-error'', and that theorem is unconditional.  It is
not, however, elementary in the sense the input list suggests, and it is
not among the machine-verified components.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{(d) A route that avoids (b) and (c) entirely.}  The
theorem needs only an \emph{upper} bound on the carry-free mass, so any
one of the $J(k)$ level conditions may be discarded.  Discarding level
one and using the levels $i=2,\dots,J(k)+1$ instead, every frequency
vector has $i_{0}\ge2$, hence $F\ge Q$ by \eqref{eq:Frange}: part~(b)
never arises, \eqref{eq:hyperbola} is not needed, and the carry-free
proportion of the band is $\le2^{-J(k)}(1+o(1))$ as before.  The only
cost is the requirement $J(k)+1\le k$, absorbed into \eqref{eq:Jk}.  The
band-Mertens input of (c) is still needed --- it is what gives the band
its mass $\asymp1/k$ --- but no statement about primes in short intervals
is.

\smallskip
\textbf{[A7]}  Independently of which route is taken,
\emph{Proposition~\ref{prop:equi} is false as stated at level one}: its
conclusion asserts the density $\prod_{i}|I_{i}|$, whereas
\eqref{eq:hyperbola} --- which is Lemma~\ref{lem:measure}, confirmed
numerically in the remark following it, and asserted in
\S\ref{sec:density} to be ``an exact statement, not a model'' --- gives
$\sigma_{\nu}(I_{1})\ne|I_{1}|$ at bounded $\nu$.  The two statements of
the paper contradict each other.  The correct form is
\[
\#\{p\in\mathcal R_{\nu}:\{n/p^{k-i+1}\}\in I_{i}\ (1\le i\le J(k))\}
=\Bigl(\sigma_{\nu}(I_{1})\!\!\prod_{2\le i\le J(k)}\!\!|I_{i}|+o(1)\Bigr)
\#\mathcal R_{\nu},
\]
uniformly for $\nu\le T_{0}$, together with Proposition~\ref{prop:equi}
unmodified for $t>T_{0}$.  For \S\ref{sec:assembly} only the upper bound
matters, and $\sigma_{\nu}([0,\tfrac12))\le\sigma_{1}
=\log(3/2)/\log2=0.5850\ldots<1$ (decreasing to $\tfrac12$), so the
carry-free proportion is $\le1.171\cdot2^{-J(k)}$ --- a change of
absolute constant only.

\subsection*{B.7\quad The case table}

Fix a band $k\le K$, a fine block $\mathcal Q=(Q,\rho Q]$, a level index
$i\le J(k)$, and a frequency $\mathbf h$ with $0<\|\mathbf h\|_{\infty}\le
H$.  Set $U:=Q^{1/3}$ in Lemma~\ref{lem:vaughanA}, so that the Type~I
range is $M\le U^{2}=Q^{2/3}$, i.e.\ $L=Q/M\ge Q^{1/3}$, and the
bilinear range is $Q^{1/3}/2\le M\le Q^{2/3}$, reduced by
Lemma~\ref{lem:typeIIsym} to $Q^{1/3}/2\le M\le Q^{1/2}$.  Then
$x=\log F/\log N$ with $\log N\ge\tfrac13\log Q(1-o(1))$ in both
families, so by \eqref{eq:Frange}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:xrange}
0\ <\ x\ \le\ \frac{\log H+(J+1)\log Q}{\tfrac13\log Q(1-o(1))}
\ \le\ 3J+4,
\qquad\text{hence}\qquad r=\max(2,\lceil x\rceil+1)\le3J+5=r_{\max}.
\end{equation}
Both hypotheses on the slack parameter
$\varsigma=r(\log\varrho+8)/\log N$ hold.  For $r\ge3$,
$\varsigma\le\dfrac{r_{\max}(\log\varrho+8)}{\tfrac13\log Q(1-o(1))}
\ll\dfrac{(\log\log n)^{4/3}}{(\log n)^{\varepsilon_{n}}}=o(1)\le
\tfrac1{16}$ by \eqref{eq:logQ}.  For $r=2$ the requirement
$\varsigma\le x/4$ is exactly $\log F\ge8\log\varrho+64$, i.e.\
$F\ge e^{64}\varrho^{8}$, which holds throughout the range $F\ge T_{0}$
of \S B.6(a) by \eqref{eq:T0}.

Under the top-level exclusion of Proposition~\ref{prop:equi} --- the
route of \S B.6(d), and the one the paper takes --- only row~1 of the

the indexing of \S B.0 (Remark~\ref{rem:bridge}).  Rows~2 and~3 are
retained for two reasons: the enumeration is exhaustive only with them,

\noindent\emph{The Kusmin--Landau range.}  \textbf{[A8]}
\S\ref{sec:schedule} describes the alternative to the derivative-test
range as ``the Kusmin--Landau range ($F\le L^{\eta}$: the phase is nearly
stationary\ldots)''.  Lemma~\ref{lem:KL} requires $\|f'\|\ge\delta>0$,

$\gg L$, i.e.\ nothing.  Kusmin--Landau enters this paper only as the
engine inside the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:vdc2}, and it is
Lemma~\ref{lem:measure} --- the parenthetical's own content --- that
covers the nearly stationary regime.  The sentence should name the band
geometry, not Kusmin--Landau; and, by \textbf{[A8$'$]}, it should name
the threshold $T_{0}$ of \eqref{eq:T0} rather than $L^{\eta}$.

\subsection*{B.8\quad The two displayed inequalities of \S\ref{sec:schedule}}

\emph{First display.}  With $r=r_{\max}=3J+5$ and
$J=\lceil(\log\log n)^{1/3}\rceil$, Lemma~\ref{lem:typeIB} gives
\[
\eta'\ \ge\ 2^{-r_{\max}-1}=\exp\bigl(-(3J+6)\log2\bigr)
\ \ge\ \exp\bigl(-c(\log\log n)^{1/3}\bigr),\qquad c:=5\log 2,
\]
the last step being the inequality
$(3J+6)\log2\le c(\log\log n)^{1/3}$, i.e.\
$3J+6\le5(\log\log n)^{1/3}$, which holds for $n$ large because
$J\le(\log\log n)^{1/3}+1$ gives $3J+6\le3(\log\log n)^{1/3}+9$.
\textbf{Verified}, with $2^{-J-3}$ replaced by
$2^{-3J-6}$; only the constant $c$ changes.  \textbf{[A6]}
\S\ref{sec:schedule} takes ``$r=J+3$''.  The correct bound is
$r\le3J+5$: the Type~I family runs down to $L=Q^{1/3}$ (Vaughan with
$U=Q^{1/3}$ delivers $M\le U^{2}=Q^{2/3}$), so
$x=\log F/\log N$ can be three times
$\log F/\log Q\le J+1+o(1)$, not once.
The schedule is unaffected because $2^{-3J}$ and $2^{-J}$ are both
$\exp(-O((\log\log n)^{1/3}))$.

\emph{Second display.}  For $k\le K$, \eqref{eq:logQ} gives
$\log Q\ge\tfrac12(\log n)^{\varepsilon_{n}}$, and $L\ge Q^{1/3}$ gives
$\log L\ge\tfrac16(\log n)^{\varepsilon_{n}}$.  Hence
\[
L^{\eta'}\ \ge\ \exp\Bigl(\tfrac16(\log n)^{\varepsilon_{n}}
e^{-c(\log\log n)^{1/3}}\Bigr),
\]
which is the displayed inequality with $c'=\tfrac16$.  For the second
half, $(\log n)^{\varepsilon_{n}}
=\exp(\varepsilon_{n}\log\log n)=\exp\bigl((\log\log n)^{2/3}\bigr)$,
so
\[
\log\bigl(L^{\eta'}\bigr)\ \ge\
\tfrac16\exp\Bigl((\log\log n)^{2/3}-c(\log\log n)^{1/3}\Bigr)
\ =\ \exp\bigl((\log\log n)^{2/3}(1+o(1))\bigr),
\]
whereas $\log\bigl((\log n)^{A}\bigr)=A\log\log n$.  Since
$\exp\bigl((\log\log n)^{2/3}(1+o(1))\bigr)/\log\log n\to\infty$, we get
$L^{\eta'}\gg(\log n)^{A}$ for every fixed $A$, uniformly in $k\le K$.
\textbf{Verified.}

\emph{The trailing claim.}  ``$\log L\gg\log Q\gg(\log
n)^{\varepsilon_{n}}=\exp((\log\log n)^{2/3})$, which dominates
$2^{J}=\exp(O((\log\log n)^{1/3}))$.''  The second $\gg$ and the identity
are correct (\eqref{eq:logQ} and the computation just made).  The first
$\gg$ is a typo: $\log L\le\log Q$ always, and what holds is
$\log L\ge\tfrac13\log Q$, i.e.\ $\log L\asymp\log Q$.  \textbf{[A9]}
With that reading the sentence is correct, and it is the inequality that
makes the whole schedule work: $\log Q$ is doubly exponential in
$\log\log\log n$ while $2^{J}$ is only singly exponential in
$(\log\log n)^{1/3}$.

\subsection*{B.9\quad Assembly of the appendix, and the flag list}

Collecting: fix $k\le K$ and $\mathbf h$ with
$0<\|\mathbf h\|_{\infty}\le H$.  On every fine block $\mathcal Q$ on
which $F\ge T_{0}$ (cases 1--2 of \S B.7),
Lemma~\ref{lem:vaughanA} with $U=Q^{1/3}$ reduces
$\sum_{p\in\mathcal Q}e(\Psi_{\mathbf h}(p))$ to
\[
\frac{Q}{U}+(\log Q)^{2}\max_{M\le Q^{2/3}}\mathcal T_{M}
+(\log Q)^{2}\max_{Q^{1/3}/2\le M\le Q^{1/2}}\mathcal B_{M},
\]
the last range by Lemma~\ref{lem:typeIIsym}.  The first term is
$\le|\mathcal Q|\cdot\varrho Q^{-1/3}\ll|\mathcal Q|(\log n)^{-B-1}$ by
\eqref{eq:logQ}.  For the second, Lemma~\ref{lem:typeIB} bounds each
inner sum by $N^{1-\eta'}$ with $N\asymp L/\varrho$, so
$\mathcal T_{M}\ll M\,(L/\varrho)^{1-\eta'}
\ll|\mathcal Q|\,N^{-\eta'}$.  For the third,
Lemma~\ref{lem:typeIIB} gives
$\mathcal B_{M}\ll Q(\log Q)^{5/2}\bigl(L^{-1/2}+(Fj)^{-1/10}
+M^{-2^{-r_{\max}-2}}\bigr)$; here $L^{-1/2}\le Q^{-1/6}$,
$(Fj)^{-1/10}\le T_{0}^{-1/10}\le(\log n)^{-B-4}$ by \eqref{eq:T0}, and
$M^{-2^{-r_{\max}-2}}\le(\log n)^{-B-4}$ by \S B.8 together with
$M\ge Q^{1/3}/2$.  By \eqref{eq:logQ} every one of these is
$\ll(\log n)^{-B-1}$, hence
$|\sum_{p\in\mathcal Q}e(\Psi_{\mathbf h}(p))|\ll|\mathcal Q|
(\log n)^{-B-1}$, and \eqref{eq:blocksum} yields \eqref{eq:weyl}.  Where
$F<T_{0}$, \S B.6(b) applies instead --- or, on the route of \S B.6(d),
that case does not occur.

Feed this into the Erd\H{o}s--Tur\'an inequality of
Appendix~\ref{app:ET} in dimension $J(k)$ with $H=(\log n)^{2}$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:ET} and Remark~\ref{rem:ETuse}).  Its first term is
\[
\ll\ \pi(P)\,J\epsilon_{H}
\ \ll\ \pi(P)(\log\log n)^{4/3}(\log n)^{-2}
\ \ll\ \pi(P)(\log n)^{-1};
\]
its outer factor $J$ on the exponential-sum term is
$\exp\bigl(O(\log\log\log n)\bigr)$; and the sum
over $\mathbf h$ carries the weight
\[
\sum_{0<\|\mathbf h\|_{\infty}\le H}\ \prod_{i}(1+|h_{i}|)^{-1}
\ \ll\ (3\log H)^{J}
=\exp\bigl(O((\log\log n)^{1/3}\log\log\log n)\bigr),
\]
which is dominated by the saving
$\exp\bigl(-\exp((\log\log n)^{2/3}(1+o(1)))\bigr)$ of \S B.8 with
overwhelming margin.

It remains to check that $B=2$ in \eqref{eq:weyl} already suffices.  The
band contains $N\gg P/\log n$ primes while $\pi(P)\asymp Pk/\log n$, so
$\pi(P)/N\ll k\le K=(\log n)^{1-\varepsilon_{n}}$, and the
exponential-sum term of Theorem~\ref{thm:ET}, measured relative to $N$,
is at most
\[
J\,(3\log H)^{J}\,\frac{\pi(P)}{N}\,(\log n)^{-B}
\ \ll\ \exp\bigl(u-u^{2/3}-Bu+O(u^{1/3}\log u)\bigr),
\qquad u:=\log\log n,
\]
using $K=e^{u-u^{2/3}}$ and $J(3\log H)^{J}=e^{O(u^{1/3}\log u)}$.  This
is $\le e^{-u}=(\log n)^{-1}$ precisely when
$(2-B)u\le u^{2/3}-O(u^{1/3}\log u)$, which at $B=2$ reads
$0\le u^{2/3}-O(u^{1/3}\log u)$ and holds for $n$ large because
$u^{1/3}\gg\log u$.  Together with the first term this gives
Proposition~\ref{prop:equi} --- as amended by \textbf{[A7]} --- with
$o(1)\le(\log n)^{-1}$, uniformly in $k\le K$.

\section{A weak $J$-dimensional Erd\H{o}s--Tur\'an inequality}\label{app:ET}

The inequality proved here is the weak one: log-power losses are
tolerated, and the sharp Beurling--Selberg extremal functions are not
used.  What is proved is enough for \S\ref{sec:schedule}, where the
losses are absorbed with room to spare (\S B.9).

Throughout, $\|y\|$ denotes distance to the nearest integer,
$e(y)=e^{2\pi iy}$, functions on $\mathbb R/\mathbb Z$ are identified with
$1$-periodic functions on $\mathbb R$, and for $f\in L^{1}(\mathbb
R/\mathbb Z)$ we write $\hat f(h)=\int_{0}^{1}f(y)e(-hy)\,dy$.  An
\emph{arc} is a set $I=[\alpha,\beta)\subseteq\mathbb R/\mathbb Z$ of
length $|I|=\beta-\alpha\in[0,1]$.

\subsection*{C.1\quad The one-dimensional approximants}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:fourier1}
For every arc $I$ and every $h\in\mathbb Z$,
$\bigl|\widehat{\mathbf 1_{I}}(h)\bigr|\le\dfrac{1}{1+|h|}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For $h=0$ the left side is $|I|\le1$.  For $h\ne0$,
$\widehat{\mathbf 1_{I}}(h)=\int_{\alpha}^{\beta}e(-hy)dy
=\dfrac{e(-h\alpha)-e(-h\beta)}{2\pi ih}$, so the left side is
$\le\dfrac{1}{\pi|h|}$; and $\pi|h|\ge1+|h|$ because
$(\pi-1)|h|\ge\pi-1>1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Fej\'er-type kernel]\label{lem:kernel}
Let $\theta>0$, $s\in\mathbb Z_{\ge1}$, and let
$\phi_{\theta}:=\theta^{-1}\mathbf 1_{[-\theta/2,\theta/2]}$ on
$\mathbb R$.  Put $\Phi:=\phi_{\theta}^{*2s}$ (the $2s$-fold convolution).
Then $\Phi\ge0$, $\int_{\mathbb R}\Phi=1$,
$\operatorname{supp}\Phi\subseteq[-s\theta,s\theta]$, and
\[
\hat\Phi(\xi)=\Bigl(\frac{\sin\pi\theta\xi}{\pi\theta\xi}\Bigr)^{2s},
\qquad\text{so}\qquad
0\le\hat\Phi(\xi)\le\min\bigl(1,(\pi\theta|\xi|)^{-2s}\bigr).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Non-negativity, total mass and support are immediate from the
corresponding properties of $\phi_{\theta}$ and the fact that
convolution adds supports.  The Fourier transform of $\phi_{\theta}$ is
$\int_{-\theta/2}^{\theta/2}\theta^{-1}e(-\xi u)du
=\frac{\sin\pi\theta\xi}{\pi\theta\xi}$, and convolution multiplies
transforms.  The final bound uses $|\sin|\le1$ and $|\sin z/z|\le1$.
\end{proof}

The case $s=1$ is the classical Fej\'er kernel (its transform is the
triangle $(1-|\theta\xi|)_{+}$ up to rescaling); $s>1$ is used only to
make the transform decay fast enough that truncation at level $H$ costs
$O(1/H)$.

\begin{proposition}[One-dimensional one-sided approximants]
\label{prop:onesided}
Let $H\ge3$ be an integer and set
\[
s:=\lceil\log H\rceil,\qquad \theta:=\frac{2}{\pi H},\qquad
w:=s\theta=\frac{2s}{\pi H},\qquad
\epsilon_{H}:=2w+\frac1H\le\frac{4\log H+6}{H}.
\]
Assume $H$ is large enough that $w\le\tfrac18$.  Then for every arc $I$
there are real trigonometric polynomials $\chi^{\pm}_{I}$ of degree
$\le H$ with
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] $\chi^{-}_{I}\le\mathbf 1_{I}\le\chi^{+}_{I}$ pointwise on
$\mathbb R/\mathbb Z$ (hence also $\chi^{+}_{I}\ge0$);
\item[(ii)] $\bigl|\hat\chi^{\pm}_{I}(0)-|I|\bigr|\le\epsilon_{H}$;
\item[(iii)] $\bigl|\hat\chi^{\pm}_{I}(h)\bigr|\le\dfrac{1}{1+|h|}$ for
$0<|h|\le H$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $\Phi$ be as in Lemma~\ref{lem:kernel} with these $\theta,s$, and
periodize it; since $2w\le\tfrac14$ its periodization is supported in
$[-w,w]$ modulo $1$.

\emph{Majorant.}  If $|I|+2w\ge1$ put $\chi^{+}_{I}:=1$; then (i) holds,
(iii) is vacuous, and $\hat\chi^{+}(0)=1\le|I|+2w$, giving (ii).
Otherwise let $I^{+}:=[\alpha-w,\beta+w)$, an arc of length $|I|+2w<1$,
and $T^{+}:=\mathbf 1_{I^{+}}*\Phi$.  Then $0\le T^{+}\le1$; and for
$y\in I$ we have $y-u\in I^{+}$ whenever $|u|\le w$, so
$T^{+}(y)=\int\Phi=1$.  Thus $T^{+}\ge\mathbf 1_{I}$.  Also
$\hat T^{+}(h)=\widehat{\mathbf 1_{I^{+}}}(h)\hat\Phi(h)$, so
$\hat T^{+}(0)=|I|+2w$ and, by Lemma~\ref{lem:fourier1} and
Lemma~\ref{lem:kernel}, for $h\ne0$
\[
|\hat T^{+}(h)|\le\frac{1}{\pi|h|}\,(\pi\theta|h|)^{-2s}.
\]
In particular $|\hat T^{+}(h)|\ll|h|^{-2s-1}$, so the Fourier series of
the continuous function $T^{+}$ converges absolutely to it.  Put
\[
\tau:=\sum_{|h|>H}|\hat T^{+}(h)|
\le\frac{2}{\pi(\pi\theta)^{2s}}\sum_{h>H}h^{-2s-1}
\le\frac{2}{\pi(\pi\theta)^{2s}}\cdot\frac{1}{2s\,H^{2s}}
=\frac{1}{\pi s}\,(\pi\theta H)^{-2s}=\frac{4^{-s}}{\pi s}\le\frac1H,
\]
the last step because $\pi\theta H=2$ and
$4^{-s}\le4^{-\log H}=H^{-\log4}\le H^{-1}$.  Now set
$\chi^{+}_{I}:=\sum_{|h|\le H}\hat T^{+}(h)e(hy)+\tau$.  It is a real
trigonometric polynomial of degree $\le H$; it satisfies
$\chi^{+}_{I}\ge T^{+}\ge\mathbf 1_{I}$ because the omitted part of the
series is at most $\tau$ in absolute value; $\hat\chi^{+}(0)=|I|+2w+\tau$,
which gives (ii); and $\hat\chi^{+}(h)=\hat T^{+}(h)$ for $0<|h|\le H$,
so (iii) follows from $|\hat T^{+}(h)|\le|\widehat{\mathbf 1_{I^{+}}}(h)|$
and Lemma~\ref{lem:fourier1}.

\emph{Minorant.}  If $|I|\le2w$ put $\chi^{-}_{I}:=0$; then (i) and (iii)
are clear and $|\hat\chi^{-}(0)-|I||=|I|\le2w\le\epsilon_{H}$.  Otherwise
let $I^{-}:=[\alpha+w,\beta-w)$ and $T^{-}:=\mathbf 1_{I^{-}}*\Phi$.  Then
$0\le T^{-}\le1$ and $\operatorname{supp}T^{-}\subseteq I$, so
$T^{-}\le\mathbf 1_{I}$; and $\hat T^{-}(0)=|I|-2w$.  With $\tau^{-}$
defined as $\tau$ was, and by the same estimate $\tau^{-}\le1/H$, set
$\chi^{-}_{I}:=\sum_{|h|\le H}\hat T^{-}(h)e(hy)-\tau^{-}$.  Then
$\chi^{-}_{I}\le T^{-}\le\mathbf 1_{I}$, $\hat\chi^{-}(0)=|I|-2w-\tau^{-}$,
and (iii) holds as before.
\end{proof}

\subsection*{C.2\quad Products}

\begin{lemma}[Telescoping]\label{lem:telescope}
Let $a_{\nu},b_{\nu},c_{\nu}$ be reals with $0\le a_{\nu}\le c_{\nu}$ and
$b_{\nu}\le a_{\nu}$ for $1\le\nu\le J$.  Then
\[
\prod_{\nu}c_{\nu}\ \ge\ \prod_{\nu}a_{\nu}
\ \ge\ \prod_{\nu}c_{\nu}-\sum_{\nu}(c_{\nu}-b_{\nu})
\prod_{\mu\ne\nu}c_{\mu}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The first inequality is monotonicity of the product of non-negative
numbers.  For the second, telescope:
\[
\prod_{\nu}c_{\nu}-\prod_{\nu}a_{\nu}
=\sum_{\nu=1}^{J}\Bigl(\prod_{\mu<\nu}a_{\mu}\Bigr)(c_{\nu}-a_{\nu})
\Bigl(\prod_{\mu>\nu}c_{\mu}\Bigr).
\]
Every factor is non-negative, and $\prod_{\mu<\nu}a_{\mu}\le
\prod_{\mu<\nu}c_{\mu}$, $c_{\nu}-a_{\nu}\le c_{\nu}-b_{\nu}$.
\end{proof}

\subsection*{C.3\quad The inequality}

\begin{theorem}[Weak $J$-dimensional Erd\H{o}s--Tur\'an]\label{thm:ET}
Let $J\ge1$ and let $H\ge3$ be an integer with $w\le\tfrac18$ and
$J\epsilon_{H}\le1$, notation as in
Proposition~\ref{prop:onesided}.  Let
$x_{1},\dots,x_{N}\in[0,1)^{J}$ and let
$B=\prod_{\nu\le J}I_{\nu}$ be a box, each $I_{\nu}$ an arc.  Then
\[
\Bigl|\#\{i\le N:x_{i}\in B\}-N\operatorname{vol}(B)\Bigr|
\ \le\ C\Bigl(N J\,\epsilon_{H}
+J\!\!\sum_{0<\|\mathbf h\|_{\infty}\le H}\ \prod_{\nu\le J}
\frac{1}{1+|h_{\nu}|}\ \Bigl|\sum_{i\le N}e(\langle\mathbf h,x_{i}\rangle)
\Bigr|\Bigr)
\]
with $C$ absolute.  Since $\epsilon_{H}\ll(\log H)/H$, this is the
asserted inequality up to the two log-power losses $\log H$ and $J$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write $\chi^{\pm}_{\nu}:=\chi^{\pm}_{I_{\nu}}$,
$A:=\#\{i:x_{i}\in B\}=\sum_{i}\prod_{\nu}\mathbf 1_{I_{\nu}}(x_{i,\nu})$,
and for $\mathbf h\in\mathbb Z^{J}$,
$\Sigma(\mathbf h):=\sum_{i\le N}e(\langle\mathbf h,x_{i}\rangle)$,
$W(\mathbf h):=\prod_{\nu}(1+|h_{\nu}|)^{-1}$,
$\mathcal E:=\sum_{0<\|\mathbf h\|_{\infty}\le H}W(\mathbf h)|\Sigma(\mathbf h)|$.

\emph{A product expansion.}  Let $g_{1},\dots,g_{J}$ be real
trigonometric polynomials of degree $\le H$ with
$|\hat g_{\nu}(h)|\le\gamma_{\nu}/(1+|h|)$ for all $|h|\le H$ and some
$\gamma_{\nu}\ge0$.  Then
$\prod_{\nu}g_{\nu}(y_{\nu})
=\sum_{\|\mathbf h\|_{\infty}\le H}\bigl(\prod_{\nu}\hat g_{\nu}(h_{\nu})
\bigr)e(\langle\mathbf h,\mathbf y\rangle)$, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:expand}
\Bigl|\sum_{i\le N}\prod_{\nu}g_{\nu}(x_{i,\nu})
-N\prod_{\nu}\hat g_{\nu}(0)\Bigr|
\le\Bigl(\prod_{\nu}\gamma_{\nu}\Bigr)\,\mathcal E .
\end{equation}

\emph{Upper bound.}  Apply this with $g_{\nu}=\chi^{+}_{\nu}$.  By
Proposition~\ref{prop:onesided}(iii) and, at $h=0$,
$\hat\chi^{+}_{\nu}(0)=|I_{\nu}|+O(\epsilon_{H})\le1+\epsilon_{H}$, we may
take $\gamma_{\nu}=1+\epsilon_{H}$, so
$\prod\gamma_{\nu}\le(1+\epsilon_{H})^{J}\le e^{J\epsilon_{H}}\le e$.
Also
\[
\Bigl|\prod_{\nu}\hat\chi^{+}_{\nu}(0)-\operatorname{vol}(B)\Bigr|
=\Bigl|\prod_{\nu}\bigl(|I_{\nu}|+\delta_{\nu}\bigr)
-\prod_{\nu}|I_{\nu}|\Bigr|
\le J\epsilon_{H}(1+\epsilon_{H})^{J-1}\le e\,J\epsilon_{H},
\]
where $|\delta_{\nu}|\le\epsilon_{H}$, by the same telescoping as in
Lemma~\ref{lem:telescope} (each factor is at most $1+\epsilon_{H}$).
Since $\mathbf 1_{B}\le\prod_{\nu}\chi^{+}_{\nu}$ by
Proposition~\ref{prop:onesided}(i) and Lemma~\ref{lem:telescope},
\[
A\le\sum_{i}\prod_{\nu}\chi^{+}_{\nu}(x_{i,\nu})
\le N\operatorname{vol}(B)+e\,NJ\epsilon_{H}+e\,\mathcal E .
\]

\emph{Lower bound.}  By Lemma~\ref{lem:telescope} applied pointwise with
$a_{\nu}=\mathbf 1_{I_{\nu}}(y_{\nu})$, $c_{\nu}=\chi^{+}_{\nu}(y_{\nu})$,
$b_{\nu}=\chi^{-}_{\nu}(y_{\nu})$,
\[
A\ \ge\ \sum_{i}\prod_{\nu}\chi^{+}_{\nu}(x_{i,\nu})
-\sum_{\nu=1}^{J}\ \sum_{i}\bigl(\chi^{+}_{\nu}-\chi^{-}_{\nu}\bigr)
(x_{i,\nu})\prod_{\mu\ne\nu}\chi^{+}_{\mu}(x_{i,\mu}).
\]
The first sum was bounded from below in the previous paragraph by
$N\operatorname{vol}(B)-e\,NJ\epsilon_{H}-e\,\mathcal E$.  For each
$\nu$, the inner sum is of the form treated by \eqref{eq:expand} with
$g_{\nu}=\chi^{+}_{\nu}-\chi^{-}_{\nu}$ and $g_{\mu}=\chi^{+}_{\mu}$ for
$\mu\ne\nu$; here $|\hat g_{\nu}(h)|\le2/(1+|h|)$ for $0<|h|\le H$ by
Proposition~\ref{prop:onesided}(iii), and
$\hat g_{\nu}(0)=\hat\chi^{+}_{\nu}(0)-\hat\chi^{-}_{\nu}(0)
\le2\epsilon_{H}\le2$, so $\gamma_{\nu}=2$ and
$\prod_{\mu}\gamma_{\mu}\le2e$.  Hence
\[
\sum_{i}\bigl(\chi^{+}_{\nu}-\chi^{-}_{\nu}\bigr)(x_{i,\nu})
\prod_{\mu\ne\nu}\chi^{+}_{\mu}(x_{i,\mu})
\le N\cdot2\epsilon_{H}(1+\epsilon_{H})^{J-1}+2e\,\mathcal E
\le 2e\,N\epsilon_{H}+2e\,\mathcal E .
\]
Summing over $\nu$ and combining,
$A\ge N\operatorname{vol}(B)-3e\,NJ\epsilon_{H}-3e\,J\,\mathcal E$.
Together with the upper bound this gives the theorem with $C=3e$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:ETuse}
Two remarks on the two losses.  (a) The factor $\log H$ inside
$\epsilon_{H}$ comes from taking $s=\lceil\log H\rceil$ convolutions, which
is what makes the truncation tail $\tau$ drop below $1/H$; with $s$
bounded the tail is bounded below by an absolute constant and the
construction fails.  It is removed by Vaaler's polynomials, which we do
not need.  (b) The factor $J$ on the exponential-sum term comes from the
minorant, which is not non-negative and so is handled by
Lemma~\ref{lem:telescope} rather than by a product.  In
\S\ref{sec:schedule} both losses are absorbed: $J\ll(\log\log n)^{1/3}$
and $\log H\ll\log\log n$, while $H=(\log n)^{2}$, so
$NJ\epsilon_{H}\ll N(\log n)^{-1}$ and the factor $J$ is dominated by the
saving of \S B.8 with room to spare.
\end{remark}
