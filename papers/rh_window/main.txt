                  The Riemann hypothesis up to height 634.5,
                         checked by the Lean kernel
                                               Samuel Lavery

                                        Draft, 29 September 2026


                                                   Abstract
          We give a proof, checked by the kernel of the Lean 4 proof assistant against Mathlib’s
      definition of the Riemann zeta function, that every nontrivial zero ρ of ζ with | Im ρ| ≤ 634.5
      lies on the critical line Re s = 12 , and that every such zero with 0 < Im ρ ≤ 634.5 is a simple zero
      of the Riemann ξ-function. A multiplicity-weighted count of 366 zeros in the window is also
      checked, so there are exactly 366 zeros with 0 < Im ρ ≤ 634.5. The proof follows Turing’s scheme.
      Sign changes of the Hardy Z-function certify zeros on the line, and an argument-principle count
      certifies that there are no others. Every numerical fact enters as a rational inequality whose proof
      the kernel checks. The only axioms used are Lean’s three standard ones, and the development
      does not use native code evaluation. We also derive the instance T = 634.5 of the predicate
      riemannZeta.RH_up_to of the PrimeNumberTheorem+ project, which at the revision we use
      records the numerical verifications of Platt, of Gourdon–Wedeniwski and of Platt–Trudgian as
      statements without proofs. The height is small by the standards of numerical verification. The
      contribution is a verification whose trusted base is the Lean kernel and the definitions, not
      numerical software. The development consists of 367 generated certificate modules (546 MB of
      Lean source), and it builds on a laptop under a documented memory limit.


1     Introduction
Numerical verification of the Riemann hypothesis (RH) up to a height T means proving that every
zero β + iγ of ζ with 0 < γ ≤ T has β = 12 . The method in use since Turing [10] has two
parts. Zeros on the critical line are located by sign changes of the real-valued Hardy function
Z(t) = eiθ(t) ζ( 12 + it). An independent count of all zeros up to height T then shows that the list
is complete. The current record is due to Platt and Trudgian [7], who “verify numerically, in a
rigorous way using interval arithmetic, that the Riemann hypothesis is true up to height 3 · 1012 ”.
    These computations are rigorous in the sense of interval arithmetic, and they run in ordinary
software. Formal libraries use their conclusions as hypotheses. At revision 21998bb the PrimeNum-
berTheorem+ (PNT+) project [8] states three such results in Lean, each closed by sorry:
 theorem Platt_theorem : riemannZeta.RH_up_to 30610046000 := sorry
 theorem GW_theorem    : riemannZeta.RH_up_to 2445999556030 := sorry
 theorem PT_theorem_1 : riemannZeta.RH_up_to 3e12 := sorry

This paper gives a kernel-checked proof of the same kind of statement at a much smaller height.

Results. All theorems below are checked by the Lean kernel, version 4.34.0, with Mathlib at tag
v4.34.0. Their axiom footprint is exactly [propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound].
    1. Every nontrivial zero with | Im ρ| ≤ 634.5 has Re ρ = 12 (Theorem 1).

                                                       1
    2. Every nontrivial zero with 0 < Im ρ ≤ 634.5 is a simple zero of ξ (Theorem 3), and the
       multiplicity-weighted count in that window is 366 (Theorem 2).

    3. PNT+’s riemannZeta.RH_up_to (1269/2) holds (Theorem 4).

    4. Mathlib’s RiemannHypothesis follows from one explicitly named hypothesis about the paired

       The hypothesis itself is not proved here beyond height 634.5.

Scope. Nothing here is new as mathematics. Zeros of ζ in this range were computed, and shown
to lie on the critical line, in the 1930s; see [9] for the history. 634.5 is also tiny next to 3 · 1012 : the
window holds 366 zeros, against about 1.2 · 1013 . It lies far below every height that PNT+ records
as a theorem without proof (3.06 · 1010 and above). The one contribution is the trusted base of
a classical computation. That base shrinks to the Lean kernel and to Mathlib’s definitions of ζ,
Γ, log, cos and π. The numerical data enters only as rational witnesses that the kernel re-checks.
Section 6 discusses how the cost grows with height.


2     Statements and trust base
2.1    Definitions
Mathlib defines riemannZeta on all of C. At the pole s = 1 it takes a fixed value that plays no
role here. The nontrivial zeros are
 def ZD.NontrivialZeros : Set C :=
   { s : C | 0 < s.re ∧ s.re < 1 ∧ riemannZeta s = 0 }

Mathlib’s completedRiemannZeta is Λ(s) = π −s/2 Γ(s/2)ζ(s). The development uses the Riemann
ξ-function in the form

                        ξ(s) = s(s−1)
                                  2   Λ0 (s) + 21 = 12 s(s − 1) π −s/2 Γ(s/2)ζ(s),

where Λ0 is Mathlib’s entire part completedRiemannZeta0 . The multiplicity of a zero is the order
of vanishing of ξ, ZD.xiOrderNat z := analyticOrderNatAt riemannXi z.

2.2    Main theorems
Theorem 1 (the window). Every nontrivial zero ρ of ζ with | Im ρ| ≤ 634.5 satisfies Re ρ = 12 .
 theorem CriticalLinePhasor.DVP.online_below_1269o2 :
     ∀ ρ ∈ ZD.NontrivialZeros, |ρ.im| ≤ 1269/2 → ρ.re = 1/2


Theorem 2 (the census). The number of zeros with 0 < Re s < 1 and 0 < Im s ≤ 634.5, counted
with multiplicity, is 366.
 def stripZeroCountMult (T : R) : N :=
   Σ ρ ∈ stripZeroFinset T, ZD.xiOrderNat ρ
 theorem CriticalLinePhasor.DVP.stripZeroCountMult_1269o2 :
     stripZeroCountMult (1269/2) = 366

Here stripZeroFinset T is the finite set of nontrivial zeros with 0 < Im ρ ≤ T .

Theorem 3 (simplicity). Every nontrivial zero ρ with 0 < Im ρ ≤ 634.5 is a simple zero of ξ.
 theorem VerifiedWindow.xiOrder_eq_one_below_1269o2 {ρ : C}
     (hρ : ρ ∈ ZD.NontrivialZeros) (hpos : 0 < ρ.im) (hle : ρ.im ≤ 1269 / 2) :

    Theorems 2 and 3 together say there are exactly 366 distinct zeros with 0 < Im ρ ≤ 634.5. In
the open strip, π −s/2 Γ(s/2) and 12 s(s − 1) have no zeros, so these are also simple zeros of ζ. We
state this in prose; the compiled statement is about ξ.

Theorem 4 (PNT+’s predicate). With PNT+’s definitions (file IEANTN/ZetaDefinitions.lean
at revision 21998bb)
 noncomputable def riemannZeta.zeroes : Set C := {s : C | riemannZeta s = 0}
 noncomputable def riemannZeta.zeroes_rect (I J : Set R) : Set C :=
   {s : C | s.re ∈ I ∧ s.im ∈ J ∧ s ∈ zeroes}
 noncomputable def riemannZeta.RH_up_to (T : R) : Prop :=
   IsEmpty (riemannZeta.zeroes_rect (Set.Ioo 0.5 1) (Set.Icc 0 T))

we have
 theorem VerifiedWindow.pnt_RH_up_to_1269o2 : riemannZeta.RH_up_to (1269 / 2)


    The proof derives it from Theorem 1. A zero in PNT+’s rectangle is a nontrivial zero with
| Im s| ≤ 634.5 and Re s > 21 .

2.3    What is trusted
    • Trusted: the Lean 4 kernel (v4.34.0). The Mathlib definitions of ζ, Γ, log, cos, sin, π and
      analytic order at tag v4.34.0. For Theorem 4 only, the three PNT+ definitions displayed
      above.

    • Axioms: #print axioms reports exactly [propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound] for
      every theorem in this section. The dependency closure contains no sorry and no user
      axiom. None of the ladder, station, census or segment modules uses native_decide, so
      Lean.ofReduceBool does not appear.

    • Untrusted: the Python programs that generate the certificate modules. They choose the
      station heights, the rational phase stand-ins and the rational bounds. A wrong choice makes
      a module fail to compile. It cannot make a false statement compile.

The proof is Turing’s scheme carried out inside the kernel. We describe it in standard terms;

At a station t the quantity Re e−iφ ζ( 12 + it) is bounded below or above using the Euler–

The station’s conclusion is a single inequality between rational numbers, checked by norm_num. A
station is therefore a large but elementary certificate. At t = 150.5 it runs to 22,162 lines.

3.3   The count
Completeness comes from the argument principle. Call a height T good when a certificate shows
Re ζ(σ + iT ) ≥ 15 for 21 ≤ σ ≤ 2 (re_pos_at_1269o2). That certificate comes from an Euler–
Maclaurin lower bound with N = 1000 on seven σ-intervals (re_zeta1269o2_lower). At a good
height, the continuous logarithm of ζ along the top of the standard contour stays on the principal
branch. This gives |S(T )| = | arg ζ( 12 + iT )|/π < 12 . The compiled argument-principle iden-
tity (classicalSContour_eq_Smult_add_offLine) reads N (T ) = θ(T )/π + 1 + S(T ) with N (T )
counted with multiplicity. Combined with the enclosure of θ(634.5), it pins the integer N (634.5)
to 366 (Theorem 2).

3.4   Exhaustion and simplicity
The ladder is 367 stations 10 ≤ t0 < t1 < · · · < t366 ≤ 634.5 with G(ti )G(ti+1 ) < 0 for ev-
ery i (LadderData1269o2, discharged by ladder1269o2Data_holds). It gives 366 distinct zeros
on the line. Any further zero in the window, on or off the line, or any zero of order at least
two, would push the weighted count above 366. The same finite-set argument proves Theorem 1
(online_below_1269o2_of_ladder) and Theorem 3. Zeros with negative imaginary part follow
by conjugation.

3.5    Rungs
The window was built in 31 rungs, with top heights 58, 63, 74, 81, 91, 109, 126, 144.5, . . . , 607.5, 634.5.
Each rung has its own good height, segment certificate, census and exhaustion. The final theorems
use only the top rung. The bottom rung, online_below_58, is the smallest slice. It uses 13 stations
and is suitable for a quick check (Section 5).

Let η(s) = n≥1 (−1)n−1 n−s = (1 − 21−s )ζ(s) for Re s > 0. The development represents the paired

series k (2k + 1)−s − (2k + 2)−s as a sum of planar vectors, laneChart3D σ y at s = σ + iy. It
      P

proves that this vanishes for 0 < σ < 1 exactly when ζ(σ + iy) = 0 (fiber3D_vanishes_iff_zero).
The factor 1 − 21−s vanishes only on Re s = 1. The named hypothesis is
 def CarrierReadout.OneVanishingPerHeight3D : Prop :=
   ∀ (y σ 1 σ 2 : R), 0 < σ 1 → σ 1 < 1 → 0 < σ 2 → σ 2 < 1 →
     laneChart3D σ 1 y = 0 → laneChart3D σ 2 y = 0 → σ 1 = σ 2

and the compiled theorem is
 theorem OneAssumptionRH.riemannHypothesis_of_oneAssumption
     (h : CarrierReadout.OneVanishingPerHeight3D) : RiemannHypothesis

where RiemannHypothesis is Mathlib’s statement. The proof is short. By the functional equation
and conjugation, a zero at σ+iy comes with a zero at (1−σ)+iy (fiber3D_vanishes_at_partner).
The hypothesis then forces σ = 1 − σ. The bridge RHBridge.no_offline_zeros_implies_rh
converts the result to Mathlib’s form. The hypothesis restricted to |y| ≤ 634.5 is a theorem
(CarrierReadout.oneVanishingPerHeight3D_below), by Theorem 1. Above that height it is not
proved here.

5       Engineering and reproducibility
Size. The window rests on 367 generated station modules totalling 546.2 MB of Lean source.
Around them sit 31 rungs of census, segment and exhaustion modules, the rational log
and square-root tables to 4000, and the contour-integral machinery. A Python program
(tmp/att271_station_emitter.py in the repository) emits the station modules from witness
tables. It is untrusted.

Throttling. The station modules dominate the cost. At default parallelism, which here is one
module per core on 12 cores, the build exhausted a 32 GB laptop. Every build reported here used
 env LEAN_NUM_THREADS=2 nice -n 10 lake build <Module>

which caps Lake at about two concurrent modules.

Checking. In the directory papers/rh_window/, the file Check.lean imports the capstones. It

Build state. In the working tree used for this draft, Lake’s traces marked some station oleans
as out of date with respect to their sources. Before we stopped that rebuild, 60 of them had been
re-checked from the current sources without error. Importing an olean does not re-run the kernel
on its proofs. A reader should therefore certify the artifact by one of two routes: a complete build
from a clean checkout, or a replay of the kernel over the oleans with lean4checker. Neither has
been run for this draft.

to T is N (T ) ≈ 2π

7    Related work
Numerical verification. Platt and Trudgian [7]: “We verify numerically, in a rigorous way using
interval arithmetic, that the Riemann hypothesis is true up to height 3·1012 .” The methods descend
from Turing [10], with corrections by Lehman [5]; see Booker [2] for an account.

Formal libraries. PNT+ [8] defines riemannZeta.RH_up_to and states the verifications of Platt,
of Gourdon–Wedeniwski and of Platt–Trudgian with sorry (Section 1). On the Lean community
Zulip, in January 2026, the PNT+ discussion “N(T)” asked whether Mathlib or PNT+ had ma-
chinery to define the zero count N (T ). The reply was that it did not yet exist there. The definitions
zeroes and zeroes_rect used in Theorem 4 were proposed in that discussion. Loeffler and Stoll [6]
formalize ζ and Dirichlet L-functions in Mathlib, including a formal statement of RH. In Isabelle,
Eberl’s entry [3] “builds upon the results about formal and analytic Dirichlet series to define the
Hurwitz ζ function ζ(a, s) and, based on that, the Riemann ζ function ζ(s)”. We found no entry
in the Archive of Formal Proofs that verifies zeros of ζ.

Zeros in proof assistants. Alpöge and Furman [1] prove that “at least two thirds of the nontriv-
ial zeros of the Riemann zeta function, counted with multiplicity, are simple and lie on the critical
line”. They provide a Lean 4 formalization that includes the Riemann–von Mangoldt counting
formulas. That result concerns proportions of zeros at large height, not the location of individual
zeros. A recent upload by Islam [4] states that “the Real part is proved, for ζ to height 3 × 1012
on the certificate of Platt and Trudgian”. It takes the numerical certificate as an input rather than
checking it in the kernel. Several public repositories formalize Hardy’s theorem or zero counting in
Lean without placing individual zeros.

Priority. We searched the sources above, the PNT+ discussion on the Lean Zulip and the Archive
of Formal Proofs. We found no earlier proof-assistant verification of RH up to any positive height.
The search is not exhaustive. We make no stronger claim.
