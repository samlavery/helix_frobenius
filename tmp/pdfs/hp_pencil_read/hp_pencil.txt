    The Gram double pencil: a spectral census for the zeros of ζ,
                and an Euler-anchored regulator


                                                   2026-08-14


                                                   Abstract
         We attach to each window of the critical line a pair of Hankel matrices computed by
     contour integration of ξ ′ /ξ, and prove that the number of zeros off the critical line in the
     window equals the negative inertia of the first matrix (Theorem 2); the remaining spectral
     data of the pair counts the zeros with multiplicity, their distinct support, and their ordinates
     (Theorem 5). Both statements are unconditional. The Riemann Hypothesis is thereby
     equivalent to a Stieltjes-type positivity condition, window by window, in a quotient chart
     obtained from the functional equation (Proposition 6). We then construct, in the half plane
     of absolute convergence and with no reference to zeros, an Euler-anchored double pencil
     which is unconditionally positive semidefinite (Proposition 8), and prove that its spectrum
     can be transported isospectrally by an explicit finite-dimensional regulator whenever a bank
     response matrix is nonsingular (Theorem 9). The remaining obligations are stated precisely
     in §8.

Remark 3. The hypothesis n ≥ m is necessary: an off-line pair is invisible to a Hankel matrix
smaller than the distinct support count. Since µ0 (W ) is the number of zeros in W with mul-
tiplicity and m ≤ µ0 (W ), the choice n := ⌈µ0 (W )⌉ + 1 is admissible and computable from the
bank alone.
Remark 4 (collisions). If a conjugate pair approaches R, the corresponding negative eigenvalue
tends to 0− and at collision becomes a null direction; the invariant through such an event is
the inertia, not any individually ordered eigenvalue. Consequently the correct target is non-
negativity Hn ⪰ 0 (equivalently n− = 0), never strict positivity: the n − m structural nulls and
the collision nulls are expected.

6    Warp covariance: fixed labels, moving readout
Theorem 9 fixes the abstract spectrum {λa } of St . It does not assert that these numbers
are zero heights: at the Euler anchor they are the Gauss nodes of the prime-clock measure
(0.96, 2.72, 5.64, 8.80 for s0 = 1.5, N = 4), whereas the terminal chart must produce ordinates
such as 14.13, 21.02, 25.01. The two are reconciled by a readout map, and the distinction must
be part of the statement.

such that the physical spectrum seen in the chart at time t is

Proposition 13. Assume Proposition 8, Theorem 9 along the transport, and Hypothesis 12.
Then ZW ⊂ R: every zero of A in W is real.

Proof. S0 is Hermitian, so spec S0 ⊂ R; the regulated transport is a unitary conjugation, so
spec St = spec S0 for all t. By (W2), ϕ1 (spec S0 ) ⊂ R; by (W3) that set is ZW .

Remark 14 (what carries the content, and the vacuity risk). In this formulation the flow con-
tributes rigidity of the labels, and the readout map carries the physics. Consequently (W1) and
(W3) are not formalities: a readout map with free parameters could carry any spectrum to
any other, making the scheme vacuous. The mathematical content of the transport is exactly
the assertion that the map determined by the bank — and by nothing else — lands on the
zero ordinates, which is obligation (T) of §8 in coordinate form. Condition (W2) is where the
functional equation must act: reality of the abstract spectrum is transferred to reality of the
physical ordinates only if the readout is real on reals.


7     The conditional theorem
Hypothesis 15 (PSD). For every window W of a tiling of R and n = ⌈µ0 (W )⌉ + 1, Hn (W ) ⪰ 0;
equivalently (Proposition 6) both Hankel matrices of the even moments are positive semidefinite
in the quotient chart.

       n− ≡ 0;

Proof. By Theorem 2, Hn (W ) ⪰ 0 with n ≥ m(W ) forces q(W ) = 0: no conjugate pair of
zeros of A lies in W . Nonreal zeros occur in conjugate pairs with equal real part, hence both
members lie in the same window of the tiling (for a pair on a boundary, apply the argument to
a shifted tiling). Every nontrivial zero of ζ corresponds to a zero of A lying in some window,

Hermitian-definite on ran Hn under Hypothesis 15.

8    Remaining obligations
Theorem 16 is conditional on Hypothesis 15. By the rigidity of the moment problem, any
pairing whose Gram matrix reproduces the moments µj+k (W ) is the counting pairing of (1), so
its positivity is equivalent to the conclusion; a proof of Hypothesis 15 must therefore proceed
from the bank side, and the following are the precise steps.

 (T) Contour transport. Continue the matrix-entry identities (not a claimed positive sum)

 (N) Pencil neutrality. Show that the transport can be regulated so that Dℓ does not move

     along the transport, which is a finite-dimensional, bank-computable condition; it holds

     C det(Hn − λHn ), C ̸= 0, rather than entrywise equality.

(W) Warp covariance. As stated in Hypothesis 12: the control u = M −1 b must be an
    admissible deformation of the registered bank, and the readout family ϕt must satisfy
    (W1) bank determination, (W2) reality and injectivity, and (W3) terminal identification
    with the zero ordinates. The invariant of the transport is the label set {λa }; the physical
    ordinates are ϕt (λa ), and it is the readout, not the flow, that must be shown to land on
    ZW .

 (C) Collisions. At spectral collisions replace the scalar conditions by Pa F (u)Pa = 0 on the
     degenerate eigenspace, treating the deformation piecewise between collisions; these are
     exactly the rank-drop events of Corollary 7.

    Granted (T), (N), (W), (C), the chain runs forward: the Euler-anchored pencil is positive
semidefinite unconditionally (Proposition 8); its spectrum is transported isospectrally and re-
mains real (Theorem 9); spectral equivalence identifies it with the window pencil; Theorem 5
identifies the latter’s spectrum with the zeros; Theorem 2 converts positivity into q ≡ 0; and
Theorem 16 concludes.
