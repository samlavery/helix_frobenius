A -- ADJUDICATION of block 1's CEIL section
==============================================================================
  Block 1 printed: 'CEILING of the absolute-tail window method: |x| <= 30.000000'
  and asserted that the method caps because T(a) is a constant while L1 decays.
  TWO DEFECTS, both mine.

  (i) The 30.0 is the TOP OF THE BINARY SEARCH RANGE, not a measurement.  T(a)
      UNDERFLOWS to exactly 0 in float64 for a >= 0.75, so C(a,x) - 2T(a) became
      literally C(a,x) = Rhat(2x) and the 'ceiling' was the target itself.
      evidence: T(0.75) = 9.765e-11, T(1.00) = 0.000e+00 (exact 0 in float64)

  (ii) The BINDING CONSTRAINT IS THE OTHER WAY ROUND.  R inherits Phi's DOUBLE
       exponential decay, so T(a) is astronomically small already at moderate a:
         T(0.30) = 2.076592e-04
         T(0.40) = 2.759876e-05
         T(0.50) = 2.030779e-06
         T(0.60) = 7.372868e-08
         T(0.75) = 9.765172e-11
       The tail is NOT what limits the method.  What limits it is that a POLYNOMIAL
       central bound cos z >= P(z) has P(z) -> -infinity, while the true central
       integral decays like e^{-pi x/2}.  No polynomial can track that, so the
       CENTRAL bound caps the reach.  Corrected statement, and it is a property of
       the polynomial-central-bound instrument class, not of the target.
